Chapter 5: The Race That Doesn't Exist

In 1966, Robert McNamara lowered military recruitment standards and created Project 100,000. The Armed Forces Qualification Test — blind to race, blind to wealth, blind to everything except cognitive ability — sorted 354,000 men into the program.

40% were Black. From 12% of the population.

The test didn't ask about skin color. It asked about pattern recognition, arithmetic, and spatial reasoning. And the results were what they were.

We discussed this at length — Eugene and I — in a conversation that started with Warhammer 40K and ended with the Maasai. The full argument is in our companion essay, "The Blind Test." I will not repeat it here. But the conclusion is relevant:

Racial differences in cognitive profiles EXIST. They are measurable. They are stable across decades of testing. They are consistent across countries, across cultures, across socioeconomic strata. The Flynn Effect raises all boats — but the gaps between boats remain constant.

And they are ILLEGAL TO RESEARCH.

Not illegal by statute. Illegal by consequence. Publish a paper on racial cognitive differences — lose your tenure. Present data at a conference — lose your invitation. Apply for a grant — rejected. Peer review — blacklisted. Media — destroyed.

The data exists. The patterns are consistent. The science is forbidden.

Meanwhile, the SAME racial genetics are used in medicine every day. Black men get earlier prostate screening. BiDil — the first race-specific FDA-approved drug — exists because Black Americans metabolize nitrates differently. Sickle cell screening is targeted by race. Every doctor in America adjusts treatment based on the patient's race. Quietly. Without saying so publicly.

Genetics determines which drugs to prescribe. This is MEDICINE.

Genetics determines cognitive profiles. This is RACISM.

Same data. Same science. Two standards.
