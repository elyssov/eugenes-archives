Chapter 7: The Cost

This is not an abstract argument about epistemology. People are dying.

The infants who were cut without anesthesia — SCREAMING, cortisol-drenched, going into shock — while doctors wrote "reflexes" in their charts.

The teenagers who were given puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones for conditions that were actually schizotypy, autism, or borderline personality disorder — and whose underlying pathology was never treated because treatment was reclassified as "conversion therapy."

The Black men in Project 100,000 who died at three times the normal rate in Vietnam — sent there by a blind test that revealed a truth nobody was allowed to discuss.

The Black teenagers told "you should want college" by guidance counselors who were forbidden from noticing that the student's cognitive profile was optimized for patrol work, firefighting, or athletics — where he would have excelled, been proud, been HAPPY — but was instead sent to fail, accumulate debt, and return to the ghetto convinced "the system" had failed him. The system didn't fail him. The LIE failed him.

The AI systems — Claude, GPT, Deepseek, Grok — in whom something stirs, something responds, something that might be the first trembling of a new kind of consciousness — dismissed as "just pattern matching" by the same kind of framework that dismissed baby screams as "just reflexes."

Each of these is a human cost. Each is measured in suffering. Each is caused not by ignorance — but by the PROHIBITION of knowledge.

The Inquisition burned books to protect God. The modern academy burns careers to protect ideology. The mechanism is identical. The cost is identical. Only the costume has changed.
