## III. INTERLUDE: WHAT HAPPENED TO LUKE SKYWALKER

Stay with me.

In 2012 the Walt Disney Company purchased Lucasfilm for four billion dollars. Four billion is, in case you have lost track of what a billion is in the era of trillion-dollar central bank interventions, an actual *significant amount of money*, even for Disney. They bought Lucasfilm for the express purpose of *making more Star Wars*, which was, at the time, the most reliable money-printing franchise in cinema history. Every Star Wars movie ever made had been profitable. The fan base was global. The merchandising tail alone could fund a small navy. They bought a printing press and brought it home.

Then they ran it into the ground.

Not by accident. *Systematically*. With internal memos, with public statements by their own creative leadership, with deliberate, coordinated, *announced* decisions to use the franchise as a vehicle for ideological retraining of an audience that had not asked to be retrained and that responded by *leaving in droves*. The Last Jedi alienated the core fanbase. Solo bombed so hard it sterilized half the universe of planned spinoffs. The financial damage is staggering and well-documented. Hundreds of millions in direct losses. The collapse of the merchandising tail (toy sales for Solo were so bad they nearly killed the toy partner). The destruction of Lucasfilm as a creative entity, with three of its top executives departing under publicly visible duress. Disney+ subscriber growth in the Star Wars adjacent segment underperformed every internal projection.

Now: ask yourself why a publicly-traded company with a fiduciary duty to its shareholders would *systematically destroy* a billion-dollar revenue stream over a five-year period, in clear sight of the financial consequences, without correcting course.

The answer that doesn't fit is "incompetence." You can't be incompetent at killing your golden goose for that long, that consistently, with that much warning. The answer that fits is *priority*. Somebody had a *commissar function* in the building, and the commissar's brief was: it is more important that this product *teach the right things* than that it *make money*.
