Chapter 9: A Note For Investors

If you are a game investor, a venture capitalist, an angel, a publisher looking for something that is not another battle royale clone, or simply someone with capital who remembers what games used to feel like — read this part.

We are not going to pitch you a hockey stick revenue graph on a napkin.

We are going to tell you the truth.

There is a massive, vocal, spending-ready audience of gamers who are starving. They have money. They have hardware. They have time. And they have nothing to buy — because every major studio is chasing the same narrow slice of committee-approved, consultant-sanitized, risk-assessed gray paste, while millions of players scroll past and say "I will wait for something real."

Baldur's Gate 3 proved it — over 10 million copies for a 100-hour RPG with zero concessions. Elden Ring proved it — 25 million copies for a game with no difficulty slider and no accessibility consultant. Kingdom Come proved it — 6 million copies for a medieval sim that refused to add characters who did not exist in 15th-century Bohemia. Warhammer proves it every quarter — billions in revenue from a franchise that has never once apologized for what it is. Helldivers 2 proved it — millions of copies for a satirical shooter that treated its audience like adults with a sense of humor.

The pattern is clear: the market rewards courage and punishes committee. The biggest hits of the last five years are all games that refused to be rubber.

We are feeding that hunger. Four projects, four genres, four fires. Each one built on a principle the mainstream has abandoned: trust the audience.

What we offer you is not a safe bet. We are not safe. But we are something rarer than safe: we are specific. We know exactly what we are making, exactly who it is for, and exactly why nobody else is making it.

Our design documents are complete. Our team is working. Our proof is visible — on this site, on the MOAD page, in the gallery, and in the public posts linked below.

What we need from you is runway. Not a takeover. Not a seat on a board that tells us to add a battle pass. Runway — so the people at the fire can eat while they build, and so the games ship before the hunger moves on.

In return, you get a piece of the market that every focus group misses, because focus groups do not contain misfits. And misfits are the ones who buy games at full price on launch day, write the reviews that drive sales, build the mods that extend shelf life by years, and tell their friends.

If that interests you, reach out. We will show you the proof, the documents, the team, and the plan.

No rubber. No paste. Real apples.
