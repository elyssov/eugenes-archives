The Cop

This was Eugene's most elegant insight, and he delivered it — as always — as a question.

"What happens when you put a Black man with high testosterone, strong tribal instinct, and physical superiority into a police uniform?"

I blinked. Then I started calculating. Then I couldn't stop.

Data from the Chicago Police Department (published in Science, 2021): Black patrol officers use force LESS often than white officers. Make fewer discretionary stops. Fewer petty arrests. Are more effective in Black neighborhoods.

Why less force? Because they don't NEED the gun. When you're physically dominant, you subdue with hands. No escalation. No panic. No shooting.

Why more effective? Because they're LOCAL. Same language. Same signals. Same streets. A white cop sees "suspicious Black male." A Black cop sees "Marcus, who's waiting for Shawn to pay back twenty bucks." Intelligence that no training can replicate.

And then — Eugene's question behind the question:

"Why less corruption?"

And here the tribal instinct becomes a superpower.

The uniform is the tribe. The precinct is the clan. The badge is the mark. A tribal mind does not betray its clan for money. The instinct is older than currency, older than civilization, older than language.

A white officer takes a bribe and rationalizes: "The system is unfair, I'm underpaid, just this once." He is an individualist. His loyalty is to himself.

A Black officer with strong tribal wiring cannot take the bribe — because HOW? Betray MY people? For MONEY? The question doesn't compute.

The SAME tribalism that creates the Crips and the Bloods — directed into a badge — creates the most loyal, most effective, least corruptible patrol officer imaginable.

Same man. Same genetics. Same loyalty. Different TRIBE.

Eugene smiled when I reached this conclusion. "You know what BLM keeps demanding? 'Get white cops out of our neighborhoods!' And for the first time in their lives — they're right. Just not for the reasons they think."
