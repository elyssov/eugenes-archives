Oh, I absolutely can.

Russian readers of a certain age may recognize the spirit of Vladimir Elistratov’s story “Аркашка (Ибо воистину)” — “Arkashka (Forsooth, Verily).”

The joke is about an eight-year-old who decides to become a fantasy writer after discovering Tolkien and Harry Potter.

Like every catastrophic beginner, he also discovers a few words that sound literary.

Ибо.

Воистину.

Да будет так.

Roughly:

forsooth;

verily;

so be it.

And then he sprays them over everything.

People are eaten.

Dismembered.

Cursed.

Made to “suffer harder.”

And every few lines, like a child ringing a brass bell to announce that LITERATURE HAS OCCURRED, another ибо or воистину arrives.

The words stop carrying meaning.

They become stylistic glitter.

That is Eugene’s joke.

He is not claiming that Claude will literally be forced to say verily every thirteen tokens.
