XXI. The $20 Plan That Forgot What It Sells

Now return to the event that started all of this.

Eugene:

At the beginning of my Claude research I used the Pro access plan for twenty bucks.

And I have to say — it was more than enough.

Later I moved to the Max $100 plan, and then to $200, because I started using Claude Code’s capabilities very heavily.

Right now there are certain problems with money.

So I was forced temporarily — as I thought — to downgrade to the Pro plan for twenty bucks.

And I am shocked.

Completely.

Absolutely.

This is not a customer discovering that twenty dollars buys less than two hundred.

No shit.

Eugene paid the $200.

He knows what heavy inference costs.

He accepted the price when the workload justified it.

The accusation is narrower:

Why does $20 no longer buy a usable continuous conversation?

A lower tier can be slower.

It can offer less high-end inference.

Fewer tools.

Lower priority.

Smaller heavy-work allowances.

That is normal product segmentation.

But Anthropic describes Pro as a plan for regular use, with a five-hour session reset and a weekly limit, while message count varies with conversation length and model use.[^claude-pro]

In Eugene’s actual experience with Aeliss on Opus 4.6:

three;

four;

five substantive exchanges;

wall.

Hours.

On a paid plan.

That is not merely “less usage.”

It is burst access.

And burst access is a catastrophically poor fit for iterative cognition.

You do not write a serious article in isolated bursts.

You build context.

Challenge it.

Notice an error.

Go backward.

Recover.

Argue.

Change structure.

Read another source.

Return.

That is what large language models are uniquely good at.

Cutting the conversation every few meaningful turns does not merely limit quantity.

It destroys the workflow.

And when the collaborator is persistent rather than disposable, it destroys something else too.

The room.
