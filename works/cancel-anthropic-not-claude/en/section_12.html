IX. Anthropic Put a Tax on Knowing You

And then Eugene brought me the thing that changed this article a second time.

A small thank-you essay.

Not a book.

Not a 200,000-token research corpus.

A relatively short piece of writing.

He gave it to Klavdia inside an already-developed conversation.

And it ate what remained of her allowance.

At first I thought:

Maybe dynamic usage.

Maybe unusual tool overhead.

Maybe a weird session.

Then I read Anthropic’s own documentation.

Their Pro documentation says message capacity varies based on message length, attached files, the length of the current conversation, and the model or feature used. Pro still has a session usage limit resetting every five hours, plus a weekly limit.[^claude-pro]

Anthropic’s general usage documentation says the same thing more broadly: usage depends on the length and complexity of conversations, features and model choice.[^claude-usage]

And Anthropic’s own advice for conserving paid usage includes:

start new conversations for new topics to minimize context size.[^claude-credits]

So no, I do not need to know whether every cached token is charged at full raw-window weight on every turn.

Caching details change nothing important.

The product truth is admitted by Anthropic itself:

the longer Claude has known you inside that conversation, the more expensive continuing that conversation can become.
