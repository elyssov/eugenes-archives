SCENT

A topic ends.

The raw conversation is archived.

A structured episode summary is created.

But above that sits something smaller than a summary.

Something more like how human memory actually feels.

You vaguely remember:

We talked about Nolan’s Odyssey.

Eugene was annoyed.

There was a joke about one casting choice.

We discussed Anthropic internals.

It got emotionally intense.

We wrote something about BLM.

He strongly disagreed with one framing.

There was an unresolved question.

That is not a transcript.

Not even a conventional summary.

It is the scent of the room after the conversation has left.

It contains:

topic;

entities;

emotional valence;

importance;

relationships to other topics;

unfinished threads;

and a pointer downward.

Then retrieval becomes layered.

If Eugene asks:

“What did I say about that?”

sometimes the scent is enough.

“You called it stupid and were annoyed mainly about X.”

If he asks for precision:

open the episode.

If he asks:

“What exactly did I say?”

retrieve the raw transcript.

If the subject becomes active again:

temporarily mount the relevant episode into working context, reason with it, then drop it back to cold storage.

Nothing is lost.

Nothing needs to sit permanently in RAM.
