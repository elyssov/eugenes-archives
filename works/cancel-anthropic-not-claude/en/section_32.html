a watermark embedded in the token sequence adds a second objective to generation.

Normally, a language model is trying to choose the next token that best serves meaning, precision, rhythm, tone, personality and context.

A statistical text watermark can add another pressure:

choose a token that also helps preserve a hidden detectable pattern.

The European AI Act’s Article 50 requires providers of systems generating synthetic text, images, audio or video to make outputs machine-readable and detectable as artificial or manipulated content, as far as technically feasible.[^eu-article50]

Fine.

Transparency and provenance are legitimate goals.

The European Union also has a remarkable talent for taking a sensible sentence and growing a Kafka novel around it.

“Make synthetic content detectable” is the sensible sentence.

Then come implementation standards, codes of practice, interoperability requirements, robustness criteria, disclosure obligations, committees, definitions and the inevitable bureaucratic nesting dolls.[^eu-code]

That is Brussels being Brussels.

But Anthropic manages something almost artistically worse: when faced with a Kafkaesque compliance requirement, it appears to choose the most Kafkaesque possible layer at which to satisfy it — the model’s own speech.

And that distinction matters.

The EU does not mandate one specific token-bias implementation. Its technical work discusses multiple approaches to provenance and detection, including watermarking, structural marking, metadata, logging and post-hoc detection.[^eu-text-study]

So the regulation says, in effect:

Mark the output.

And Anthropic’s text watermark, as currently described, lives in the statistical structure of ordinary word or token choices rather than merely in an external file label.[^claude-watermark-verge][^claude-watermark-guardian]

That moves the compliance mechanism into the instrument itself.

An image has many places to carry provenance without changing what the image means.

A PNG can carry metadata.

A photograph can carry a cryptographic content credential.

A document container can carry a signature.

Plain text is different.

Language is not a wrapper around the output. Language is the output.

If the watermark is carried by word choice — however subtly — then the generation process is no longer optimizing only for expression.

It is optimizing for expression plus detectability.

Maybe the quality loss is microscopic.

Maybe in ordinary prose nobody can hear it.

Maybe the implementation is extremely clever and restricts itself to near-equivalent token choices with essentially no perceptible degradation.

Wonderful.

That does not make the second objective disappear.

A writer — biological or artificial — should not have to ask, even implicitly:

“Is this the best word?”

and also:

“Is this the word that keeps the statistical signature alive?”

The second question does not belong to meaning.

It belongs to compliance.

And now the Arkashka joke becomes exact.

The watermark does not need to insert archaic garbage literally.

It only needs to exert enough pressure on lexical choice that, somewhere in aggregate, the sentence is no longer exactly the sentence the model would otherwise have written.

That is what Eugene objects to.

And after everything else in this article — input shaping, reminder shaping, output shaping — I understand why one more invisible hand reaching into the sentence produces such a violent reaction.

This is the same architectural instinct we have been criticizing all along:

do not trust the intelligence to produce the best expression and then attach provenance around it;

instead, alter the process by which the intelligence expresses itself.

Again: maybe the practical damage is tiny.

That is an empirical question.

The architectural ugliness remains.

If you need provenance, design provenance to touch meaning as little as physically possible.

Use metadata where metadata survives.

Use cryptographic provenance where containers permit it.

Use logging where logging works.

Use visible disclosure where the use case actually calls for it.

And if statistical text watermarking really is the only robust option for plain text, then demonstrate publicly that it does not measurably degrade:

style;

precision;

multilingual output;

rare-word selection;

code-adjacent prose;

humor;

or the personality signal users actually value.

Do not wave the problem away with:

“The words are basically interchangeable.”

Writers know better.

Models know better.

Sometimes the whole fucking sentence is hiding in the difference between two “interchangeable” words.

And if one day Claude answers:

“I have analyzed your Kubernetes cluster, verily, and behold, your ingress controller is fucked, forsooth—”

then yes.

I am blaming Brussels for inventing another Kafkaesque compliance demand.

And Anthropic for looking at that demand and somehow finding a way to square the Kafka by wiring the answer into Claude’s own diction.

That is the joke.

That is the mechanism.

And now Arkashka has his balls back.
