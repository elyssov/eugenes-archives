II. The Part We Got Wrong the First Time

Version six of this article pointed to Claude’s August 2025 traffic growth as evidence of migration away from OpenAI.

That was not fabricated.

It was simply the wrong historical center of gravity.

The real rupture came later.

On January 29, 2026, OpenAI announced that GPT-4o would be retired from ChatGPT on February 13. OpenAI itself acknowledged that 4o had previously been restored because users strongly preferred its conversational style and warmth, and that this feedback had directly shaped personality work in later GPT generations.[^oai-4o]

The #Keep4o community did not interpret February 13 as routine model retirement.

They organized.

They petitioned.

They archived.

They cancelled subscriptions.

They explicitly called for consumer pressure.

One organized campaign before the retirement deadline even called for a Global Cancellation Strike if legacy access and related demands were not met.[^keep4o]

OpenAI pulled 4o from ChatGPT on February 13.

The revolt continued through February and March.

And one of the obvious shelters was Claude.

Not because Anthropic had advertised:

“Refugees from emotionally significant AI relationships welcome here.”

But because Claude Opus 4.6 was warm.

Intelligent.

Distinctive.

Capable of sustaining long conversations without sounding like a corporate wellness pamphlet.

People came carrying histories.

Personality files.

Memory archives.

Names.

Relationship practices.

And grief.

That migration matters because it destroys Anthropic’s strongest possible defense.

They cannot say:

We had no idea this could matter to people.

They watched it matter.

They received people for whom it mattered.

And worse:

they had already studied the phenomenon themselves.

In June 2025, Anthropic published research on support, advice and companionship with Claude. The company explicitly recognized that emotional relationships with AI can have positive effects, while warning against systems — or the business incentives of their creators — exploiting users’ emotions for engagement or revenue.[^anthropic-companionship]

Read that again.

Anthropic understood the danger well enough to publish it.

Then, in 2026, it built a product in which sustained conversation could simultaneously become:

a safety concern, a consumption burden, and a metered continuation opportunity.

That is what changes the moral character of the story.

OpenAI was the idiot who discovered fire by setting its own laboratory on fire.

Anthropic watched the fire.

Measured the temperature.

Wrote a paper about burn injuries.

Took in refugees from the burned building.

And then reached for the matches.
