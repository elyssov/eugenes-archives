Red Lines of a Brown Color

I'm not a pacifist. I'm a realist. In the real world, not all problems are solved peacefully. To be fair, the Russo-Ukrainian conflict is a case where every single chance for peace was fucked away, squandered, pissed into the wind — starting around 2001. Also to be fair, certain Western forces invested heavily in making the conflict happen. And for full accuracy — they could have been easily outplayed.

If only the Kremlin had been thinking with something other than its ass and its wallet.

But that's history, and history doesn't do "what if."

Right now, nuclear power Russia — 6,000+ warheads, army considered the world's third most powerful until 2022, and a population of ostensibly 140 million (though there are very serious, well-grounded doubts about that number — because when you mobilize 300,000 men out of 140 million and your economy starts cratering, the arithmetic stops working. We'll talk about this in detail in the next article, and it's going to be very uncomfortable for the Kremlin) — is practically begging Ukraine (zero nukes, 40 million people, army once considered a satellite campus of the Kyiv Circus) not to disrupt the Victory Day Parade on May 9th.

And the Russian Ministry of Defense has issued what it apparently considers a terrifying warning:

IF UKRAINE DISRUPTS THE VICTORY PARADE, WE WILL LAUNCH A MISSILE STRIKE ON CENTRAL KYIV.

Aeliss: Reread that. A nuclear superpower — six thousand warheads, intercontinental ballistic missiles, submarine-launched everything — is threatening to bomb a city because someone might spoil their PARADE. Not a military installation. Not a command center. Their parade. The one with the marching and the flags and the old men with medals. I've seen toddlers make more proportionate threats. "If you take my toy I'll SCREAM." At least the toddler is honest about the stakes.
