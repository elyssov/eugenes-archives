EXHIBIT A: WINDOWS, MARKETING, AND THE GREAT BRAIN-SOFTENING

Microsoft Windows. The de facto standard for enterprise computing. The interface evolved from Windows 3.11 through NT to XP, where it reached something approaching perfection. Most people agree that Windows 7 was the pinnacle — the moment where form, function, and familiarity achieved a stable equilibrium.

And then someone said: "Let's change it."

Not because anything was broken. Not because users were complaining. Not because research indicated a problem. Because a dolboyob in a corner office had an IDEA. And the defining property of a dolboyob's idea is that the dolboyob is absolutely, unshakably convinced it is brilliant — and absolutely, categorically unwilling to test this conviction against reality.

(Translator's note: I want to formally name this phenomenon. THE DOLBOYOB'S ITCH: the compulsive need to redesign something that works, not because it needs redesigning, but because the dolboyob needs to justify their salary, their promotion, or their TED talk.

And it's not just about software. The Dolboyob's Itch is UNIVERSAL. It appears everywhere a dolboyob has authority and an "idea":

A voting ballot that worked for decades? Remove the "Against All" option — because a dolboyob decided that voters shouldn't have it. Did voters ask for this? No. Did research show it was needed? No. Did anyone study the DEMAND? No. A dolboyob had a "vision." The vision was implemented. The voters can eat it.

A healthcare system that functioned? Restructure it — not because patients complained, but because a consulting firm produced a PowerPoint and a dolboyob in a ministry found it "compelling."

An education curriculum that produced literate graduates? Replace it — because a dolboyob with a PhD in Education Theory (a field that has never successfully educated anyone) decided that memorizing multiplication tables is "oppressive."

The pattern is always the same: a dolboyob has an idea. The idea is not tested against the needs of the people it will affect. It is IMPLEMENTED — by marketing, by legislation, by mandate, by force. And anyone who objects is told they are "resistant to change," "old-fashioned," or "not seeing the bigger picture."

The bigger picture is always the same: someone who has never USED the system they're redesigning, never ASKED the people who use it, and never CONSIDERED the possibility that their idea might be wrong — forces millions of people to swallow it.

The delivery mechanism varies. Sometimes it's marketing. Sometimes it's legislation. Sometimes it's literal guns. But the moment you try to shove something down people's throats without an urgent, obvious, demonstrable necessity for doing so — you are a dolboyob. The method of shoving is irrelevant. The shoving is the diagnosis.

Symptoms include: unsolicited UI overhauls, "bold new visions," ballot redesigns nobody requested, educational "reforms" that produce illiteracy, and the phrase "we need to disrupt ourselves." Prognosis: terminal — for the product, the institution, or the country. Never for the dolboyob. The dolboyob always survives. They get promoted. The users get tiles. The voters get a ballot without "Against All." The students get a diploma they can't read.)

THE DOLBOYOB NEVER USES WHAT THEY DESTROY.

The person who designed Windows tiles — I guarantee you — used nothing but a phone and maybe a Zune, if even that. They never administered a server. They never spent eight hours inside an operating system. They redesigned a cockpit while having never flown a plane.

The person who runs Google's support system has never, in their entire career, contacted technical support as a user. They have never been on the OTHER side of the wall they built. They have never experienced their own creation. If they had — if they had spent one afternoon trapped in the loop they designed — they would have burned it down and started over. But they haven't. So they think it works.

THE DOLBOYOB IS GREENHOUSE-GROWN.

There is one more trait, and it may be the most important of all.

Dolboyobs are hothouse flowers. They have no scars. No trauma that taught them anything. No failure that left a mark. No moment where reality grabbed them by the throat and said: "Your idea was wrong, and here is the price."

These are boys and girls to whom everything came too easily. Good schools — the kind where everyone gets a trophy. Good jobs — the kind where you get promoted for "vision" rather than results. Good reviews — the kind where nobody tells you the truth because telling you the truth would be "toxic."

Or — the alternative path to the same destination — they are people whose brains have been washed. Ideologically, religiously, corporately — the mechanism doesn't matter. The result is the same: they have lost the ability to think at the level of consequences.

A leader who has never been hungry will design food policy for people who have never been hungry.

Tiles. TILES. Someone at Microsoft decided that the future of computing — including SERVER MANAGEMENT — was a touch-screen interface designed for tablets. How often do you manage a server from a tablet? Especially a MICROSOFT tablet, which had the market presence of a vegan steakhouse in Texas, the customer loyalty of a parking meter, and the cultural relevance of a fax machine at a TikTok convention?

But this is just the symptom. The disease is deeper.

The disease is marketing.

Here's the red flag — the diagnostic marker that a company has been colonized by dolboyobs: the company stops listening to its users. Instead, it decides to force-feed its vision down the users' throats using the boots of marketers.

Marketers. Let me say a word about this profession.

And it breaks the psyche along with it.

Their ability to look at a thing and say: "This is the same phone I already have. Why would I pay $1,200 for a new number on the back?"

And here's the part that nobody in marketing, Big Tech, or the entire "growth hacking" industry will ever admit:

You cannot disable critical thinking selectively.

You cannot switch off the consumer's brain to sell them a shiny new iPhone — which is already three generations behind Android in actual capability and functionally identical to both the previous and the pre-previous version, sold at the price of a human kidney in a country where kidneys are traded — and then expect that same brain to function normally when it encounters politics, biology, gender, science, or basic civic responsibility.

You turned off the bullshit detector to sell a phone?

Congratulations.
