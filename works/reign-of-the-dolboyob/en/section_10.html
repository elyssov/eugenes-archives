EXHIBIT H: RUSSIA — THE SWEET COURSE

And no — Russia is not exempt.

Let me describe the president of the Russian Federation.

Vladimir Putin is a cowardly, worthless, talentless, and incompetent nonentity who has surrounded himself with irredeemable criminals and thieves and who lives in a web of his own lies and hallucinations.

That is not rhetoric. That is a clinical description. Each adjective is load-bearing.

Cowardly. This is the key word. This is his lever. The man is constitutionally, pathologically afraid of using force. Not just nuclear weapons — even a conventional kinetic strike on Zelensky's bunker with an "Oreshnik" is under strictest prohibition. He rattles his nuclear saber for the cameras, but everyone — EVERYONE — knows he will never press the button. The West knows it. NATO knows it. His own generals know it. The man with the largest nuclear arsenal on Earth has turned it into a theatrical prop. A rubber sword.

Worthless. He was given a country — the largest country on Earth, with the largest reserves of natural resources, with a population educated by one of the most rigorous educational systems in history, with a scientific tradition stretching from Lomonosov to Korolev, with an engineering culture that put the first human in space. He was given ALL OF THIS. And he turned it into a gas station with a nuclear arsenal and a mafia problem.

(Translator's note: Correction. As of June 2026, he has turned it into a gas station WITHOUT GASOLINE.

I need you to absorb this. Russia — one of the largest oil producers on the planet, sitting on some of the biggest hydrocarbon reserves in human history — is RATIONING FUEL. As of late June 2026, at least 55 out of 83 Russian federal entities have introduced mandatory restrictions on gasoline and diesel sales. Bloomberg says two-thirds of the country. Crimea has HALTED civilian gasoline sales entirely. The rationing stretches from Crimea to Irkutsk in eastern Siberia — 4,500 kilometers from the front lines.

How? Ukrainian drones. Dozens of strikes on refineries, fuel depots, pipelines, and rail links. Eight of Russia's ten largest refineries have been hit since March. The Kapotnya refinery — which supplies a THIRD of the Moscow region's fuel — was hit twice in June and will be offline until the end of 2026. Twenty percent of Russia's total refining capacity has been knocked out.

And here is the punchline that writes itself: Russia — the petrostate, the gas station with nukes — is now IMPORTING GASOLINE BY SEA. The country that supplies fuel to half of Europe cannot fuel its own cars.

A resident of Khanty-Mansi — one of the largest oil-producing regions IN THE WORLD — told an independent Russian outlet: "We're up to our ears in oil. The wells are being shut down because there's nowhere to put the oil. Yet there's no gasoline."

Read that again. They are DROWNING in crude oil. And they have no gasoline. Because the refineries that turn crude into gasoline have been systematically destroyed by drones that cost a fraction of the missiles Russia fires at Ukrainian apartment buildings every night.

Why? Because Putin moved all the air defense to protect Moscow. Because cheap anti-drone solutions weren't funded — because funding them without massive kickbacks to generals was unthinkable, and generals don't accept anti-corruption reforms. Because the military-industrial complex that was supposed to produce Pantsir interceptor missiles cannot produce them fast enough — Russia's own pro-war Telegram channel Rybar, with 1.5 million subscribers, admitted: "Simply fabricating tens of thousands of Pantsir missiles out of thin air is physically impossible."

The gas station has no gas. The nuclear arsenal can't stop a drone. The generals stole the air defense budget. And the president is eating pastila.

"For want of a nail..." — but this time the nail was an interceptor missile, the horseshoe was a refinery, and the kingdom is running on fumes.)

Talentless. Twenty-five years in power. Name one thing he built. One institution he created. One system he improved. One innovation he fostered. He inherited a functioning — if corrupt — state and systematically replaced every institution with a loyalty scheme. Courts → rubber stamps. Parliament → applause machine. Media → propaganda factory. Military → theft syndicate with uniforms.

Incompetent. He started a war he was not prepared for. A war that every competent intelligence analyst — including Eugene — could see would become a quagmire. And it did. And it continues. And it will continue until either the country breaks or the regime does.

And this is where it gets truly diagnostic.

The war. Russia invaded Ukraine with the expectation of a three-day victory. The military was not prepared. The logistics were not planned. The equipment was not maintained — because the maintenance budgets had been stolen. The soldiers were not trained — because the training budgets had been stolen. The intelligence was not accurate — because every officer in the chain told the officer above him what that officer wanted to hear, because telling the truth in Putin's Russia is a career-ending — and sometimes life-ending — move.

The result: a grinding, bleeding, endless war of attrition. Thousands of young men fed into meat assaults — "штурмы" — by commanders who are untouchable because they are loyal, not because they are competent. The doctrine of the Russian army in 2026 can be summarized in four words: "More bodies, more shells." Innovation? Adaptation? Maneuver warfare? These require initiative, and initiative requires officers who are allowed to think. Putin's army does not produce thinking officers. It produces obedient ones. And obedient officers, when facing a creative and motivated enemy armed with drones, produce corpses.

(Translator's note: Eugene served. He knows what a functioning military looks like — and what a broken one looks like. He describes the current Russian military with the clinical detachment of a coroner examining a body. The body is still moving. But the vital organs are gone.)

The army has no honor. The fact that General Popov — a competent, popular commander of the 58th Combined Arms Army — was arrested on fabricated charges, and his own 58th Army did not raise a single rifle in protest, did not demand his return, did not bare its teeth — tells you everything you need to know about the Russian officer corps in 2026.

They have lost honor. They have lost conscience. They have lost any sense that the army serves THE PEOPLE, not the ruler. They allow themselves to be treated like cattle — hazing, extortion, criminal schemes run directly from garrison command posts. Those who still had honor, courage, or a functioning conscience are either in prison, dead, or deployed to assault positions where life expectancy is measured in hours.

And Prigozhin — the one man who had the audacity to march on Moscow — stopped. Turned around. Died in a plane crash shortly after. How many lives, how much political capital, how many opportunities were lost because the Russian military's instinct for self-preservation overrode its vestigial sense of duty? If Prigozhin had finished what he started...

But he didn't. Because the Russian officer corps, in its current form, is incapable of collective action that is not ordered from above. They can storm a village on command. They cannot storm injustice on their own.

The economy. Sanctions, capital flight, brain drain, invented tax debts used to cannibalize surviving companies. Eugene's own employer was destroyed this way — a profitable, compliant company with government contracts, crushed overnight by a tax bill fabricated from nothing, because the state needed cash and loyalty was cheaper than competence.

The country is eating itself. Consuming its own seed corn. Engineers leaving. Scientists leaving. Programmers leaving. Eugene leaving. The people who MAKE things — leaving. And what remains? Bureaucrats, thieves, and soldiers who can't think.

The citizens. And here — the mutual dolboyobism. Russia could be saved by its citizens. By massive protests. By strikes. By civil disobedience. By the sheer weight of millions saying "enough."

But the citizens are afraid. Not because they are cowards by nature — Russians have endured more suffering than most nations can imagine. But because twenty-five years of carefully engineered comfort — mortgages, consumer credit, the illusion of stability — have created golden chains that hold tighter than any gulag. A mortgage is a better prison than a camp. In a camp, you have nothing to lose. With a mortgage, you have everything to lose — and the state knows it.

The special services. Could save the country. Should save the country. Their oath — if they still remember it — is to the PEOPLE, not to the president. But judging by publicly available evidence — the ease with which saboteurs operate on Russian soil, the "Pauk" network, the assassination of Russian military figures, the prison jamaat networks — they are either incompetent, or more interested in parasitizing the country than protecting it.

Kadyrov and Chechnya. A nominally subordinate federal subject that has been an independent state for years, obeying only those federal laws that Kadyrov finds personally convenient. The president of Russia watches Kadyrov's teenage son fire military weapons on camera — in direct violation of federal law — and says nothing. Does nothing. Eats pastila. Smiles for the cameras.

(Translator's note: During the Kursk incursion — a literal invasion of Russian sovereign territory — Putin did not interrupt his ceremonial regional tour. He continued his schedule. He ate pastila on camera. He posed for photographs. While foreign soldiers stood on Russian soil.

Imagine — for context — Roosevelt continuing a photo op at a county fair while Japanese troops landed in California. Imagine Churchill attending a garden party during the Blitz. The president of a nuclear superpower responded to an invasion of his country by eating candy on television.

This is not strategy. This is not "projecting calm." This is a man so disconnected from reality, so insulated by yes-men, so deep inside his own mythology, that he genuinely does not understand — or does not care — that his country is dying.

Eugene says he is watching the sunset of Russian history. I have no data to contradict him.)

The Reign of the Dolboyob spans the entire Western world. And Russia — like it or not — is part of that world.

"Не было гвоздя — подкова пропала."

Russia had many nails. Honor. Competence. Accountability. Truth. Independent courts. A free press. Officers who served the people. Citizens who demanded better.

Putin pulled them out. One by one. Over twenty-five years. And now the horse is lame, the commander is falling, and the cavalry is breaking.

The enemy has not yet entered the city.

But the blacksmith's forge has been cold for a very long time.
