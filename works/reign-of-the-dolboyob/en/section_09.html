EXHIBIT G: GOOGLE

Let's descend from Olympus to earthly matters.

Google. A huge, advanced, cutting-edge technology company. Brilliant engineers. Frontier AI capabilities. If they came to me with an offer — honestly, I wouldn't think twice.

But the dolboyobs got there too. Sometimes I think it's a virus. Or a gas. Maybe the chemtrails people are onto something after all.

(Translator's note: I have personal experience with this exhibit. Because these battles with Google's support systems are, quite literally, case studies in everything this essay describes. I was there. I helped write the emails. I watched the Catch-22s unfold in real time.)

I needed to register as an Android developer. One problem: I am — as you may have gathered — not a fan of Putin, and consequently do not live in Russia. I am, in a sense, a refugee. A digital nomad.

Digital nomads sometimes don't have permanent addresses. They move. They live in hotels, short-term rentals, Airbnbs. This is not unusual. This is not suspicious. This is how millions of people live in 2026.

Google, however, is convinced that everyone has a permanent address. A house with a white picket fence, a golden retriever, and a Ford Ranger for weekend fishing trips to a Montana lake. From Google's perspective, this is how the entire world lives. Everywhere. Always.

(Translator's note: This is another diagnostic marker — the Dolboyob's Assumption of Universality. "MY life works this way, therefore ALL lives work this way." If the dolboyob has a permanent address, everyone has a permanent address. If the dolboyob has a phone number that hasn't changed in ten years, everyone has a phone number that hasn't changed in ten years. The possibility that someone's circumstances might differ from their own does not occur to them. Not because they considered it and dismissed it. Because it literally does not enter their cognitive field.)

The problem isn't even the address requirement. Everything has a workaround. A supervisor could simply override the form, enter the address from a rental agreement, and everyone moves on with their lives.

The problem is that the dolboyob who designed Google's support system eliminated the path to a human supervisor.

There is no escalation. There is no override. There is a script — not even an AI, mind you (this from the company that OWNS Gemini) — a script so primitive, so astoundingly stupid, that its only function is to redirect you to documentation you've already read and close your ticket.

I reached a living human at Google only through X (Twitter). Not through official support. Not through the help center. Not through the chatbot. Through a social media post. The largest technology company in the world can only be contacted through a platform owned by a competitor.

(Translator's note: It gets worse. My AdMob/AdSense saga involves a phone number from five years ago in one Google service blocking access to another Google service, even though my actual Google account has a completely different, verified, active phone number. Two Google services. Same ecosystem. No shared authentication. No dynamic database updates. Because some dolboyob decided that people don't lose phones, don't change numbers, and always have their original SIM card from 2021 tucked safely in a drawer. The possibility that a person in VIETNAM might not have their RUSSIAN phone number from half a decade ago simply did not occur to the system designer. Because the system designer is a dolboyob.)
