The Dark Knight Rises (2012): The warning signs were already there. The ENTIRE Gotham City police force — in full dress uniform — marches underground and sits there for HALF A YEAR. Underground. Six months. Shaving daily, apparently, because when they emerge they look fresh as daisies. And then they charge — on foot, in formation, UNARMED — into a line of criminals with assault rifles. And WIN. You could blame this on comic book logic. Many did. But it wasn't comic book logic. It was dolboyob logic wearing a comic book costume.

Inception (2010): And here the dolboyob fully revealed himself.

You have a premise: humans can enter OTHER PEOPLE'S DREAMS. Dreams. The most unlimited, most psychedelic, most visually infinite space the human mind can conceive. In a dream, you can build impossible geometries. Cities that fold onto themselves. Oceans that hang upside down. Forests made of memory. Rooms where physics is a suggestion and beauty has no ceiling.

Do you remember "The Cell" (2000)? Jennifer Lopez crawls through the comatose mind of a serial killer. And what does director Tarsem Singh give us? A world of staggering, hallucinatory beauty. A horse split into glass sections. A throne room made of flesh. A cape that stretches to infinity. INVENTIVE cinematography. Terrifying, gorgeous, psychedelic imagery that makes you FEEL like you are inside a broken mind.

Now — what does Christopher Nolan do with the most unlimited visual canvas in cinema history?

He builds a hotel corridor.

And a concrete bunker.

And a ski resort.

AND A CITY THAT LOOKS LIKE EVERY OTHER CITY IN EVERY OTHER ACTION MOVIE EVER MADE.

Interstellar (2014): Global food crisis. Solution: everyone becomes a farmer. Not vertical farming. Not hydroponics. Not synthetic proteins. Not genetic engineering. Not any of the seventeen technologies that ALREADY EXIST. Farmers. With pitchforks. In the future.

The masses were told Nolan is a genius. The masses ate it with a spoon and asked for seconds. Because their bullshit detectors had been removed by marketing. (See Exhibit A.)

The Odyssey (2026): And now — the masterpiece. The magnum opus of dolboyobism.

Christopher Nolan decided to adapt Homer's Odyssey. The foundational text of Western literature. A poem that has been retold for three thousand years. Written by a Greek. About Greeks. Set in Greece. Defining what it means to BE Greek.

Here is what Nolan did with it:

Casting: Matt Damon as Odysseus. Tom Holland as Telemachus. Anne Hathaway as Penelope. Lupita Nyong'o as Helen of Troy — the woman described by Homer as having golden blonde hair and dazzling white skin. Zendaya as Athena. Elliot Page as Sinon. Travis Scott as a "rapping bard" — because Nolan decided that Homer's oral poetry tradition is basically rap.

Greek actors in this Greek story about Greek people filmed in Greece: Zero. Not one. Out of thirty-plus cast members. Not a single person of Greek descent. In the most Greek story ever told.
