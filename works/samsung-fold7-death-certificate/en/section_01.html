Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7: How My Wife's Premium Flagship Died in Five Months While Samsung Blamed Her for Gravity

By Eugene Lyssovsky (@elyssovsky)

Let me tell you about the day I watched my wife's face fall.

Not from tragedy. Not from loss. From something smaller and uglier — the precise moment when a corporation looked her in the eye and said, in the politest possible Vietnamese-accented English: "This is your fault."

My wife Alena is a long-time Samsung fan. She bought the Galaxy Z Fold 7 together with a Galaxy Watch 8. Top of the line. Full Samsung ecosystem. Years of brand loyalty. She'd used Samsung flagships for years, she'd recommended them to friends, she'd defended them in arguments.

The Fold 7 was supposed to be her reward for that loyalty.

It cost roughly what a Honda ADV costs. You know — an actual vehicle. Made of metal and rubber and oil. A machine that can carry a human being through rain, heat, potholes, Vietnamese traffic, and the everyday mechanical stupidity of the physical world.

Samsung, for comparable money, sold her a phone that apparently needs to be handled like a Fabergé egg in an earthquake.

The screen first started dying in Da Lat, during a trip. May 17th. We were there from the 12th to the 19th.

We got back to Nha Trang on the evening of the 19th, dealt with other life tasks, and went to the Samsung service center today — May 22nd. Five days after the screen died.

The phone itself is about five months old. Bought in late December 2025, in Thailand, from an official Samsung store.

And then, the day before our service center visit, her Samsung Galaxy Watch 8 stopped charging. The original Samsung charger apparently died.

"No problem," we thought. The Fold 7 has a feature: wireless PowerShare, lets you charge other devices. Samsung devices charging Samsung devices. Ecosystem synergy. Marketing poetry.

The watch refused.

Samsung's own watch told Samsung's own flagship phone: "This wireless charger is not compatible."

Grenades of the wrong caliber, as we say in Russian.

So now we had two Samsung flagships — a phone with a dying screen and a watch that won't charge from its own sibling device. Full ecosystem experience.

This morning — May 22nd, 2026 — we were sitting in a Samsung service center in Nha Trang. A small coastal city, service center located in what I will politely describe as the ass end of nowhere. Watching a technician explain why Samsung would not honor the warranty on a phone that is five months old.
