There's Nothing Left to Investigate

Samsung doesn't need to "look into this." The evidence is already screaming across the internet.

Reddit is full of Fold screen failures. Samsung Community forums are drowning in complaints. According to reports citing Reddit and Samsung forums, the Fold 6/7 series has a 10-15% failure rate in the first year for inner screen issues.

Let that sink in.

Ten to fifteen percent.

In any competent manufacturing operation, this would trigger an immediate production halt and a full product recall. This isn't opinion — it's basic quality management, taught in every MBA program and Six Sigma course on the planet.

Industry standards for consumer electronics allow approximately 1,000 defects per million units — that's 0.1%. Automotive electronics are held to 20 defects per million — 0.002%. The legendary Six Sigma standard is 3.4 defects per million.

Samsung's Fold line is running at 100,000 to 150,000 defects per million.

That's not a defect rate. That's a catastrophe rate. That's 100 to 150 times above acceptable consumer electronics standards. 5,000 to 7,500 times above automotive standards.

Any company with functioning quality control would have stopped production, recalled every unit, and publicly apologized. Instead, Samsung continues selling these devices at $2,000 a pop while denying warranty claims based on irrelevant cosmetic scratches.

Vodafone customer service in the UK is reportedly "well aware of this screen fault" — they told one customer that "lots of their customers with the Fold 6 had this problem appearing after the phone is about 7-8 months old."

Green lines. Screen going green. Black bleed. Display autolysis. This isn't a mystery. This is a documented epidemic going back to Fold 3, Fold 4, Fold 5, Fold 6, and now Fold 7.
