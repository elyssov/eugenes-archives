The Real Problem

Nobody believes Samsung lacks the engineering capacity to make a stronger phone.

Nobody believes Samsung doesn't understand drops, corners, chassis strength, surface treatments, hinge stress, display layer fragility, or real-world usage.

So the problem isn't technological ignorance.

The problem is product philosophy.

Samsung took an inherently delicate concept — a folding OLED smartphone — dressed it in premium language, priced it like luxury engineering, and handed the practical risk to the customer.

The user pays flagship money for experimental fragility. Then, when reality touches the device, the company points to body marks and says: "Ah. Mechanical damage."

Sell the dream.

Collect the premium.

Let gravity happen.

Blame the owner.

Offer a repair bill large enough to hurt but not large enough to make the whole scam philosophically obvious.
