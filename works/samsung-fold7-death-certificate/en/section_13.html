Samsung Has Lost Face

There is a concept in Korean culture — 체면 (chemyeon) — that roughly translates to "face," but means something deeper: honor, dignity, the public trust that a person or institution has earned through consistent honorable behavior.

Samsung was once a symbol of Korean industrial excellence. A company that clawed its way from post-war poverty to global dominance through quality, innovation, and relentless attention to detail. A source of national pride.

What we witnessed in that service center in Nha Trang was not the behavior of that company.

We witnessed a corporation that sells a $2,000 device with a 10-15% first-year failure rate, then denies warranty coverage by pointing at cosmetic scratches on the frame that have nothing to do with the failure. We witnessed a service center that has seen so many Fold failures that the spare parts are depleted. We witnessed a product line that has been failing consistently across five generations — and is still being sold as "premium innovation."

This is not a company that has merely made a mistake. This is a company that knows its product is defective and continues to sell it anyway, while systematically denying responsibility when it fails.

In Korean terms: Samsung has not just lost face. Samsung is actively spitting in the face of every customer who trusted the brand.

이것은 수치입니다. This is shameful.

The Fold line is the most failure-prone flagship phone series in the industry. And Samsung knows it.

So here's the real demand:

Either recall the entire Fold line and fix whatever manufacturing defect is causing this epidemic of screen deaths.

Or declare unconditional lifetime warranty on inner display failures — covering everything except obvious physical damage like "someone hammered a nail through the screen."

Because right now, Samsung is selling $2,000 phones that have a 10-15% chance of dying within a year, then blaming customers for "mechanical damage" when they try to claim warranty.

That's not premium. That's fraud with a folding screen.

Because from where we were sitting — in a small service center in Nha Trang, in the midday heat, with multiple Fold devices arriving like casualties — this doesn't look like premium innovation.

It looks like a reputation collapse with a hinge.
