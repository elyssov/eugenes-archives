The Bottom Line

We've been Samsung customers. Samsung fans, even. The kind of customers companies normally want to keep.

But if Samsung responds to this with the usual corporate shrug — "mechanical damage, pay for repair" — then the conclusion is simple.

We are done with Samsung products.

Not because one device failed.

Because the company's response will reveal whether Samsung still has basic commercial decency.

If Samsung wants to sell experimental foldables, fine. Call them experimental. Price them like experimental devices. Warranty them like experimental devices.

Stop pretending that a folding OLED miracle is a rugged daily flagship when the real-world ownership experience turns into dead pixels, black bleeding, cratered aluminum, warranty refusal, and a repair bill big enough to buy a serious chunk of another perfectly functional phone.

The cruelest thing in this story isn't that my Redmi survived.

It's that my Redmi made Samsung look ridiculous.

A cheaper, second-hand Chinese phone endured harsher treatment and remained a phone.

Samsung's premium foldable, protected by a case and owned for less than half a year, became evidence.

That's not the future.

That's an invoice with a hinge.

Eugene Lyssovsky Nha Trang, Vietnam May 2026
