The Service Center

We arrived around noon. The worst heat of the day. The hour when normal Vietnamese locals hide under awnings, cling to shade, and don't go anywhere unless there's a real reason.

We had a real reason. My wife's two-thousand-dollar phone was dying.

The technician took one look at the frame and refused warranty service.

Not because of the screen.

Because of marks on the aluminum frame.

Small marks. Chips. Ugly, yes. But on the FRAME — not on the screen that was literally undergoing autolysis. Not at the point where the OLED was bleeding black. Not anywhere that would logically connect to what was happening inside the display.

Marks. On. The. Frame.

Samsung's warranty logic is beautifully primitive: if the body has scratches, everything that ever goes wrong inside the device gets thrown into the same trash bin marked "customer fault."

Convenient.

Very fucking convenient.

The repair quote: 15 million Vietnamese dong total (inner screen 12.2 million + outer screen 2 million + labor). About $600. About 50,000 rubles. Not small enough to ignore, not large enough to buy a new phone. Perfectly calibrated to remind the customer that the privilege of owning Samsung's futuristic engineering marvel includes occasionally buying a large chunk of it again.
