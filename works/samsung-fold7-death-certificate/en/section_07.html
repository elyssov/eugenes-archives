The Humiliation

Now here's where the comparison becomes ugly.

Next to Alena's dying Samsung sits my Redmi Note 14 Pro Plus.

Yes. Redmi. Not even Xiaomi proper — Redmi is an external OEM platform. A budget sub-brand. A phone made by a contract factory for a fraction of the flagship price.

I bought it second-hand.

I dropped it many times.

I'm about 180 cm tall.

Onto tile.

More than once.

A couple of times without a case.

Its glass back was intact. Its screen was intact. Its corners looked almost indecently perfect.

Then it went swimming. Fell into water. We washed it out, dried it — and the damn thing is STILL ALMOST ALIVE. The only casualty is the fingerprint sensor, which died and won't come back. The option even disappeared from Android settings. But everything else? Works.

A budget Chinese phone SURVIVED DROWNING better than Samsung's premium flagship survives EXISTING.

And here's the kicker: a brand new Redmi Note 15 Pro Plus costs 18 million dong.

Samsung's total repair quote: 15 million dong (inner screen + outer screen + labor).

For just 3 million dong more than REPAIRING the Samsung, you can buy a BRAND NEW Redmi that is technically superior in almost every measurable way:

  • Camera: Both have 200MP main sensors. Same league.
  • Battery: Redmi has 6,500mAh. Samsung has 4,400mAh. Redmi wins by 50%.
  • Charging: Redmi does 100W. Samsung does 25W. Redmi charges 4x faster.
  • Water resistance: Redmi has IP69K. Samsung has IP48. Redmi wins.
  • Glass: Both use Gorilla Glass Victus 2. Except Redmi's doesn't mysteriously crack.
  • Price: Redmi costs a quarter of the Samsung. And survives real life.

The only thing the Fold has is the folding screen. Which, as we've established, is busy decomposing.

A used Redmi that has eaten tile impacts like breakfast looks more structurally honest than Samsung's premium foldable after a few months of protected real-world use.

How?

Seriously, Samsung — how?

I refuse to believe that Samsung — one of the largest electronics manufacturers on Earth — cannot make a $2000 phone more durable than a Chinese OEM device that costs a quarter of the price. This isn't a technology problem. This isn't a "foldables are hard" problem.

This is a materials fraud problem. This is selling premium prices for garbage materials. This is "Advanced Armor Aluminum" that's actually porous casting reject.
