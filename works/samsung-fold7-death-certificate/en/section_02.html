What Happened

Let me set the scene.

I was lying on the bed. Alena was giving me a therapeutic massage — she'd been learning techniques from online tutorials. Her phone was lying open on the flat surface of the bed in front of her, playing a video where a lecturer was demonstrating pressure points on a patient's back.

Nobody was touching the phone. Nobody was bending it. Nobody was applying any pressure. It was just lying there, doing exactly what a phone is supposed to do: playing a video.

And suddenly, right as the lecturer on screen started pressing on the relevant points, the image turned green.

Then the inner display began to die. One area went defective. Then the defect spread. Then black "ink" started crawling under the glass — not like a crack, not like an impact point. Like OLED autolysis. Like watching a medical tragedy unfold inside Samsung's miracle display.

A premium screen, decomposing in real time.

And it's still decomposing. As I write this, the black bleed has spread further — now it's creeping around in a circle. The screen is literally dying in slow motion, like watching organ failure through a window.

This wasn't the proud death of a device smashed by catastrophe. This was a flagship quietly rotting while still trying to show us how to do a back massage.
