The Gap

The most insulting part isn't that the screen failed. Screens fail. Devices fail. Manufacturing defects exist. New technologies are difficult.

The insulting part is the gap between Samsung's grand mythology and the pathetic physical truth.

The mythology says: innovation, durability, premium materials, advanced engineering, next-generation foldable experience.

The physical truth says: inner display dying like spoiled algae under glass, aluminum corners cratered from ordinary life in a case, cover glass with a suspicious hairline crack, warranty refused immediately, a painful repair bill, and other Fold owners arriving at the same service center like extras in a tragicomedy about expensive design mistakes.

Samsung doesn't sell the Fold as a lab prototype.

Samsung doesn't say: "Dear customer, please understand that this device is a delicate experimental object. It folds, yes, but the price of that trick is that you must treat it with the reverence normally reserved for archaeological textiles."

No.

Samsung sells it as a phone. A daily driver. A flagship. A lifestyle object. A technological achievement.

Then reality arrives and the product suddenly wants the handling conditions of a diplomatic crystal vase.

That's the central fraud of the experience.

Not legal fraud. Something worse in consumer terms — expectation fraud.

The customer is sold confidence. The customer receives anxiety.

The customer is sold strength. The customer receives excuses.

The customer is sold the future. The customer receives a repair estimate.
