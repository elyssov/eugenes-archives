Big meanings.

Max. There are no big meanings here. There is a small sordid story — possibly invented entirely, possibly distorted from a much more banal real event — about a CEO who could have spent 500 rubles on a taxi and chose instead to spend 8 hours extracting half a month's wages from a working-class kid via the employer who pays him.

The big meaning of this post is that the LinkedIn ecosystem has rewarded the genre of invented-or-embellished business parables so consistently that practitioners have stopped checking whether their plots make logical sense. They just need to feel like the kind of thing a CEO would say. The audience has been trained to consume the shape of insight rather than the substance of insight.

Your post is the shape of insight. It is the silhouette of wisdom. It contains a setup, a complication, a resolution, and a moral. It cites a price. It references a security camera. It mentions an employee. It deploys the vocabulary of "trust" and "leadership."

It is, in every dimension that does not require actual content, a perfect LinkedIn post.

What it lacks is one functioning element of physical, financial, logistical, ethical, or human reality.
