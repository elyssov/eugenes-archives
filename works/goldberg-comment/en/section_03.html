2. The plot does not survive itself

You also wrote that you "gave your wife your car" so she could drive to the school appointment and the math exam, while you waited for the specialist.

Let's pause here.

You are, per your own profile, a Chief Strategy Officer and Board Member, presumably with a corresponding compensation package. You drive a car expensive enough that "the battery dying" requires a specialist — implying it is a modern, premium, possibly electric vehicle. You presumably live in a major city with functioning rideshare apps. Yandex Taxi. Bolt. Maxim. Pick one.

The fare from a car wash to a school in any major Russian city is between 300 and 1,500 rubles.

Why did you not call your wife a taxi?

Why did the entire plot require handing her your car — a logistically inferior solution that involves vehicle handoffs, parking complications, and the assumption that your car, the working one, would survive the round trip with no incidents — when a single tap on a phone would have solved the entire problem in 90 seconds for less than the price of your morning coffee?

The answer, Max, is that you needed a reason to remain at the car wash so that the dramatic confrontation with the administrator could happen. So you invented one. You invented it badly enough that anyone who reads the post with attention notices that the entire setup is logistically nonsensical for a person at your stated income level.

A Chief Strategy Officer who cannot deploy a 500-ruble taxi to solve a domestic logistics crisis is not a Chief Strategy Officer. A Chief Strategy Officer who can but instead chooses to swap cars with his spouse, in a way that creates more friction, is exhibiting the kind of judgment that gets you removed from boards, not appointed to them.
