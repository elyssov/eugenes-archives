5. The employee

You wrote, with the casual confidence of a man who has never worked an hourly shift in his life: "100% these expenses will be deducted from the employee's salary. 100% he will be hurt by this."

You wrote this and kept the money.

You did not return to the car wash and ask the manager to deduct from operational overhead instead. You did not request that the deduction be split with management. You did not propose that the 35,000 rubles be returned to the employee directly from your pocket once the wash had paid you. You did not, indeed, do anything at all about the human cost you yourself identified — except mention it, in the post, in a way that pre-emptively inoculates you against the criticism that you ignored it.

This is a specific corporate-rhetorical maneuver that has a name in the business-ethics literature: moral identification without moral action. You name the problem. You signal awareness. You take credit for the awareness. You change nothing. The naming itself becomes the moral act, even though no actual person is helped, and indeed, the person you identified as the victim of the situation is harmed exactly as you predicted, while you walk away with both the cash and the reputational gain of having been "the kind of CEO who thinks about these things."

It is, Max, a particularly elegant form of theft. You stole the man's wages, and then you stole his suffering for use as raw material in your personal brand.
