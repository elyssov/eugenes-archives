THE GREAT ROAMING SKELETON OF THE KORONUS EXPANSE

And then we reach another visual sin: asset reuse.

Asset reuse is normal.

Necessary, even.

Every game reuses assets.

What matters is whether the player notices the repetition as production repetition rather than as world repetition.

A chair repeating in ten hab-blocks? Fine. The Imperium mass-produces chairs.

The same cogitator pattern? Fine.

The same shrine kit? Fine.

The same industrial pipe modules? Completely normal.

But Owlcat has a particular robed skeleton statue with a sword.

This fucking gentleman is everywhere.

Hall.

Shrine.

Station.

Temple.

Void installation.

Somewhere, I swear, he is probably free-floating in space because death itself filed a request for reassignment.

At a certain point he stops being environmental art and becomes a cast member.

I expect a companion quest.

Sir Bones of StockAsset IV.

KIRA: This is where "reuse" turns into "the art department gave up hiding the seams."

Repeated assets are invisible when they belong to a kit language.

For example: if a Forge World repeatedly uses the same structural arches, maintenance alcoves, icon panels, and machine shrines, that can actually strengthen the setting. It tells you the Mechanicus has standard patterns.

But a large, distinctive statue is not drywall.

It is a noun.

A narrative object.

If the same very recognizable robed skeleton with sword appears in unrelated places, the player does not think, "Ah, a shared Imperial iconographic tradition."

The player thinks:

"Oh. It's the same mesh."

That is the fatal transition.

The object stops existing in the fiction and starts existing in the Unity project folder.

And once the player sees the project folder, the magic is dead.

There is a cheap fix, by the way.

Not "make fifty new statues."

Take the same mesh.

Mirror it.

Remove the sword.

Swap the head.

Add a book.

Add an Aquila.

Add purity seals.

Put it on a different plinth.

Break the arm.

Cover half of it with cloth.

Change scale.

Rotate.

Combine with another object.

Suddenly one base asset becomes ten environmental variants.

This is level-art camouflage 101.

If I can recognize the same dead monk from orbit, you did not camouflage hard enough.
