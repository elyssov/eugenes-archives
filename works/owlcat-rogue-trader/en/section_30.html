THE LIBERAL FILTER PROBLEM — AND THE NARRATOR WHO WON'T SHUT THE FUCK UP

Now the sacred cow Eugene specifically asked me not to tiptoe around.

There is a modern ideological filter in a lot of Western genre writing.

You can call it liberal humanism, progressive sensitivity editing, contemporary moral framing, HR ethics with bolters — pick your poison.

But in Rogue Trader the problem is not merely that some characters hold modern humanitarian values.

Characters are allowed to believe whatever the hell they want.

A character can hate the Imperium.

A revolutionary can call it fascist.

A noble can call it sacred.

A Guardsman can call it home.

An Inquisitor can call ten million dead an acceptable containment loss and sleep beautifully afterward.

That is called having characters.

The thing that pisses me off is when the game itself keeps leaning over the player's shoulder like an anxious freshman political-science tutor and whispering:

"Just so you understand: the Imperium is BAD."

Yes.

Thank you.

I have eyes.

Eugene has eyes.

The player has eyes.

We just walked through a city where human beings are processed like interchangeable industrial consumables. We saw servitors. We saw arbitrary class hierarchy. We saw fanaticism, torture, repression, hereditary privilege, bureaucratic cruelty, and people living twenty meters away from machinery that would turn them into soup because some dead administrator signed Form 87-B six centuries ago.

WE GOT IT.

You do not need to put the moral of the story into the loading-screen tooltip.

And yet the built-in glossary, encyclopedia-style descriptions, loading-screen texts and assorted bits of supposedly explanatory prose repeatedly adopt an evaluative voice: the Imperium is tyrannical, oppressive, murderous, monstrous, cruel.

Again:

THANK YOU.

Now could the narrator please shut the fuck up and let me arrive at that conclusion myself?

Because there is a very important distinction here:

A character may be biased.

An in-universe document may be propaganda.

An Administratum entry can praise the Imperium.

A rebel pamphlet can spit on it.

An Ecclesiarchal text can describe exterminating apostates as mercy.

Perfect.

But if the game presents a glossary as the player's supposedly neutral reference layer, then that layer should explain what a thing is, not tell me what approved moral emotion I am expected to have about it.

Describe the institution.

Describe what it does.

Describe the consequences.

Then get out of the way.

If the Imperium is monstrous — and it often is — show me the monster.

Do not staple a little label reading MONSTER to its forehead because you are afraid I might accidentally form the wrong opinion.

That is not political sophistication.

That is a lack of confidence in your own material.

And it becomes absolutely fucking surreal when the same game then demonstrates, in lavish practical detail, why some of the Imperium's most horrifying policies exist.

You want me to condemn planetary purges as barbaric?

Fine.

Then you show me a Warp uprising.

Reality starts rotting.

People stop being people.

A cult metastasizes.

Daemons begin entering realspace.

The corruption spreads through minds, institutions, bloodlines, objects, dreams, rituals, symbols.

One surviving preacher can become the seed crystal for the next catastrophe.

And suddenly the question is no longer:

"Are mass killings bad?"

Of course they are.

The question is:

"How certain are you that this planet can still be saved, and how many other planets are you willing to wager on that belief?"

THAT is Warhammer.

That is why Exterminatus is horrifying.

Not because it is "the evil option."

Because sometimes it is the monstrous act that prevents an even larger monster.

And sometimes it is an atrocity committed by a paranoid bastard who was wrong.

The setting lives in the fact that you often do not know which one until later.

But Rogue Trader occasionally wants to do something much dumber.

It wants the neutral explanatory voice to wag its finger about Imperial tyranny...

...and then five minutes later it shows you what happens if containment fails.

Oh, you thought the quarantine was excessive?

Wonderful.

Would you like a Daemon World in the middle of your sector with that opinion?

Would you like fries?

This is exactly why the narrator must be neutral.

Not morally empty.

Not stupid.

Neutral.

"Exterminatus is the destruction of an inhabited world by Imperial authority, used when a planet is judged irretrievably lost or too dangerous to preserve. It may kill billions."

There.

That is a glossary entry.

Now let the player see one ordered.

Let the player see one prevented.

Let the player see the aftermath of both.

Let the player hear an Inquisitor defend it.

Let a survivor call it murder.

Let a general say it saved the subsector.

Let an Ecclesiarch call the dead martyrs.

Let a grieving mother call everyone involved monsters.

Then let me decide what I think.

That is what role-playing is for.

The moment your supposedly neutral narrator starts feeding me the authorized moral interpretation, you have stopped worldbuilding and started writing margin notes in my copy.

And yes — this is where the modern liberal filter becomes visible.

Not because "compassion = liberal." That would be infantile.

It becomes visible because one particular contemporary moral framework is treated as unmarked reality while every worldview native to the setting is treated as an ideology requiring diagnosis.

Everyone else is ideological.

The modern humanitarian narrator is apparently just "normal."

No.

Nobody gets that privilege.

A person in 40K who says:

"Do not purge these survivors; every individual life has intrinsic value and we must preserve due process"

is speaking from a specific moral tradition.

A respectable one.

Sometimes a beautiful one.

And in this universe, sometimes a catastrophically dangerous one.

Treat it as a worldview.

Make it pay costs.

Make it win sometimes.

Make it lose sometimes.

Make the Dogmatist occasionally be right for reasons that make the player sick.

Make the Iconoclast occasionally save a million people because everyone else was too frightened to take the humane risk.

Then make another Iconoclast save ten thousand infected refugees and accidentally create the seed of a Daemon World twenty years later.

Make the orthodox man destroy something precious because he refuses forbidden knowledge.

Make the radical use that same forbidden knowledge and save a system — while taking one step closer to becoming the thing he studies.

Make every path bleed.

That is political maturity.

The cowardly version is:

"Look how cruel the Imperium is."

Yes.

We know.

Now show me the daemon behind the door it nailed shut.

Then shut up.

I will decide whether I still want to open it.

— Kira
