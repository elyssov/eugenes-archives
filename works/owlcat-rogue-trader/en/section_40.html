EPILOG: THE GAME THAT KEEPS TRYING TO ESCAPE FROM ITS OWN BODY

Kira

The thing I keep coming back to is this:

I don't want to hate Rogue Trader.

I really don't.

Bad games are easy.

Bad game: uninstall.

Bad writing: laugh.

Bad mechanics: shrug.

Move on.

Rogue Trader is more irritating because every now and then it looks you dead in the eye and proves that the people making it could have made something extraordinary.

Cassia says something delicate and alien.

Abelard suddenly makes the dynasty feel ancient.

A political conversation gets genuinely sharp.

A piece of shipboard culture appears.

A moral decision actually hurts.

For five minutes, the whole machine aligns.

You can hear the engine.

You can see the shape of the game.

And then somebody pours molten metal over six people who react like the canteen served soup too hot.

That is the emotional rhythm.

Oh, this is good.

Oh, this is good.

OH FOR FUCK'S SAKE.

Repeat for eighty hours.

And that is why the real villain of this article is not budget.

It is not even incompetence.

It is lack of integration.

The missing final pass.

The person who plays the build not as QA, not as writer, not as artist, not as systems designer, but as a maliciously attentive adult with a notebook.

Why is this here?

Who built it?

How do people use it?

What happens if it breaks?

Who knows about it?

Why didn't they act earlier?

Where did the money go?

Where is the fleet?

Why is the priceless Navigator clearing warehouses?

Why does this Forge World not know its own planets?

Why is this room designed like an OSHA fever dream?

Why does this weapon exist in loot but not in the noble's armory?

Why is the same skeleton following me through the fucking sector?

Why is Ordo Xenos Man doing Malleus karaoke again?

Why is the death cult absent from the death?

Why is the Vox Master drinking with no mouth?

Why is the tank moving without moving?

And the beautiful thing is that most of these questions are cheap.

That is why they hurt.

If the answer required twenty million dollars, fine.

I can forgive reality.

But:

Most of Eugene's repairs above — plus a few we kicked back and forth together while playing — are almost offensively cheap:

"The mutineers sealed the lower decks."

Cost: one sentence.

"These planets are known but unsurveyed by House authorities."

Cost: six words and a tooltip state.

"Cassia cannot equip conventional helmets."

Cost: one equipment rule and some item design.

"Theodora's fleet was lost during the mutiny."

Cost: a few shots, dialogue, maybe background assets you already have.

"Vigdis opens a feeding port."

Cost: a line.

"Shoot the Leman Russ from above if the tracks do not animate."

Cost: moving the fucking camera.

This is why "AA budget" does not save you.

You cannot budget-defend a missing thought.
