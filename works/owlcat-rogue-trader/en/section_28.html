THE CODEX IS WRITTEN FOR A FIVE-YEAR-OLD AND STILL MANAGES NOT TO EXPLAIN ENOUGH

The tooltips are another thing.

Who are they for?

They are too reductive for a veteran.

Too thin for a newcomer.

And often written with weird evaluative framing instead of simply telling you what the thing is.

If I am new to Warhammer, "Adeptus Mechanicus: technology people" is not enough.

If I already know Warhammer, it is insulting.

Build a proper encyclopedia.

Not a wiki dump.

A real in-world codex.

Weapons.

Institutions.

People.

Planets.

Ships.

Historical events.

Illustrations.

High-resolution portraits.

Good object art.

A player who wants one sentence gets one sentence.

A player who wants five paragraphs gets five paragraphs.

Everybody wins.

And yes, I am going to compare this to our own work again.

In Mysteries of Ancient Darkness we are building a proper in-game encyclopedia because the world matters to us.

Weapons get their own illustrations.

Not a grey schematic that looks like the customs declaration for a lasgun.

Actual styled artwork.

Objects get descriptions that explain what they are, how they are used, where they come from, and why the player should care.

The encyclopedia is part of the narrative delivery, not the drawer where exposition goes to die.

And we are doing this as a home-brew studio consisting, functionally, of one human developer and AI collaborators.

We have tested Unity ourselves. We know what the engine can do. Whatever else is wrong here, "Unity made us use blurry portraits and anonymous inventory diagrams" is not a serious defense.

We walked away from Unity for our own reasons.

Its inability to display a sharp picture was not one of them.

So yes, I am going to be rude about this.

If our tiny operation can sit there obsessing over whether a weapon illustration is attractive enough to make the player open an encyclopedia entry for FUN, a major licensed CRPG can perhaps find the forbidden technology required to export a portrait above potato resolution.

KIRA: There. THAT comparison needed to stay.

Because it is not merely boasting.

It is evidence of priority.

A tiny team does not have spare labor.

Every hour spent on an encyclopedia illustration is an hour not spent somewhere else.

Which means when a tiny team chooses to do it anyway, they are declaring:

"This detail matters to the product."

That is what investors should be looking for.

Not whether a pitch deck contains the phrase "scalable pipeline" seventeen times.

Whether the people making the thing know which details are identity and which are decoration.

Now, back to the guns.

And please, for the love of the Omnissiah, give weapons some identity.

Not only stats.

A Mars-pattern lasgun should have a sentence about why that pattern exists.

A plasma gun should have provenance.

A naval officer's pistol should feel naval.

A noble's dueling weapon should have a social history.

This is free worldbuilding sitting in your inventory screen.

Instead inventories in RPGs often become warehouses of anonymous arithmetic.

Rifle A: +7. Rifle B: +9 but green. Rifle C: +11 and has a proper noun.

No.

Objects are culture.

Warhammer understands this better than almost any setting.

Half the universe is built from people arguing about which exact ancient pattern of machine is holy.

Use it.
