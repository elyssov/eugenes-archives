THE FRIGATE PROBLEM: GREAT HOUSE, SMALL BOAT, NO EXPLANATION

And now — before we even leave the subject of first impressions — the ship.

Our mighty flagship.

A frigate.

A Sword-class or Firestorm-class frigate, depending on player choice.

Not a cruiser.

Not a grand cruiser.

Not a private monster modified across twelve generations.

A frigate.

And the game wants me to believe this is the principal vessel of a major Rogue Trader dynasty with planetary holdings.

Why?

Again: progression. I understand.

The player must start "small."

Fine.

Then write it.

Here is the version I would use:

Theodora begins with a proper squadron.

Grand cruiser flagship.

A pair of Lunar-class cruisers or comparable heavy vessels.

Escorts.

Transports.

One frigate acquired recently — bought, captured, inherited, confiscated from a debtor, whatever.

Then the opening mutiny spreads across the squadron.

Ships fire on each other.

One jumps into the Warp.

One blows apart.

One remains loyal but is crippled.

The flagship is lost.

The player escapes with the Warrant and key personnel.

The rescue craft reaches the frigate.

The one surviving ship.

Now you have the exact same gameplay starting point.

But the starting point means something.

You are not "mighty lord with mysteriously shit boat."

You are the survivor of a dynastic catastrophe.

You rebuild.

That is a story.

And, again, notice what I have not asked for.

I have not asked you to change the player's long-term ship progression.

I have not asked you to let us command five ships.

I have not asked you to rebuild void combat.

I have asked you to change the opening context so the exact same crappy little frigate becomes emotionally correct.

That is the difference between "give me more budget" and "let me fix your script."

KIRA: This change is almost offensively efficient.

And if some investor is still reading, highlight this paragraph.

This is the sort of design repair that makes me want to put a fluorescent vest on Eugene reading DO NOT LET THIS MAN NEAR YOUR SCRIPT UNLESS YOU WANT QUESTIONS.

It fixes:

  • why you start on a frigate;
  • why the armament is basic;
  • why the fleet is gone;
  • why the dynasty is vulnerable;
  • why restoring colonies matters;
  • why the player feels like an heir rather than a lottery winner.

Most importantly, it lets the player see what was lost.

Text telling me "House von Valancius was mighty" is database information.

Let me walk the flagship for twenty minutes.

Let me see escort icons through the void display.

Let Abelard casually mention ships by name.

Let the opening disaster destroy them.

Now, seventy hours later, when a new cruiser joins the dynasty, I do not receive "Ship Tier Increased."

I receive:

We have a fleet again.

That is ludonarrative harmony.

Same mechanics.

Better meaning.
