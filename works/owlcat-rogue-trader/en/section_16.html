THE REAL PROBLEM: THIS IS NOT A ROGUE TRADER GAME

And now we reach the center of the corpse.

The biggest problem with Rogue Trader is not the graphics.

Not the traps.

Not the frozen Leman Russ.

Not the robed skeleton who is currently filing a transfer request to Footfall.

The biggest problem is:

This game is not really about being a Rogue Trader.

It is a classic Owlcat party CRPG wearing a Warrant of Trade around its neck.

That distinction poisons everything.

A Rogue Trader is not primarily a dungeon-delving adventurer.

Can a Rogue Trader personally go on expeditions?

Of course.

Can a Rogue Trader fight?

Yes.

Can a Rogue Trader board ships?

Absolutely.

Can the tabletop campaign become a ground-combat adventure?

Sure.

But the entire point of the role is scale, privilege, politics, resources, and delegated power.

If I want to spend my life crawling through underhives personally investigating cult activity, there is another game line for that.

It is called Dark Heresy.

A Rogue Trader has a voidship.

Even our ridiculous little frigate has around 26,000 people aboard.

Thousands of armed personnel.

Void armsmen.

Officers.

Security.

Servitors.

Specialists.

And, if needed, the ability to recruit additional boarding manpower from the lower decks by offering status, pay, or simply the opportunity to become slightly less disposable.

So why the fuck am I clearing street gangs?

Why am I personally doing routine security work?

Why is the surviving head of a near-extinct dynasty walking into every ambush in the sector?

Abelard Werserian should have chained me to the captain's chair and said:

"My lord, until you produce at least one heir, you are not personally clearing a toilet blockage, much less boarding a heretic vessel."

And he would be right.

KIRA: This is the thing.

The game treats "Rogue Trader" as a dialogue permission rather than as a gameplay ontology.

You get special lines.

People call you Lord Captain.

Sometimes you can pull rank.

You own colonies.

There are resource screens.

Good.

But the minute tactical gameplay starts, your social identity evaporates.

You become Pathfinder Man again.

Six people.

Squares.

Loot.

Door.

Fight.

Door.

Fight.

Chest.

Fight.

And the title "Rogue Trader" becomes flavor text wrapped around a combat loop designed for a completely different kind of protagonist.

Imagine the same design error in another genre.

You make a game about the President of a country.

The game tells you that you control ministries, intelligence services, military forces, a diplomatic corps, and a federal bureaucracy.

Then every mission is the President personally sneaking into warehouses and shooting smugglers.

At some point you would ask whether the Secret Service had all been eaten.

That is exactly what happens here, except the scale is planets.
