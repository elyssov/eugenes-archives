THE UX: "GUESS WHAT THE BIRD-CATS WERE THINKING"

The map design and quest signposting deserve their own flogging.

Not because every game needs glowing yellow paint.

I dislike over-signposting too.

But there is a difference between "the player must pay attention" and "the player must share a nervous system with the level designer."

One of the recurring experiences in Rogue Trader is not solving an in-world problem.

It is solving Owlcat Intent.

Where is the secret chamber?

Where is Adeptus Amasecus?

Why is the quest calling a place one thing while the map calls it Martyr's Endurance?

Which side of the rotating platform contains the giant door the camera refuses to show?

Which sequence of technopriest prayer lines does the game want?

Which cult object is interactive?

Which floor tile is secretly a plot device?

At some point I started asking Kira about these roughly once an hour.

Not because I refuse to think.

Because "think" and "guess the undocumented state machine" are different verbs.

KIRA: A difficult interface respects intelligence.

A bad interface merely withholds information.

This game sometimes confuses the two.

If the map says Martyr's Endurance and a quest says Adeptus Amasecus, either:

  • show both names;
  • cross-reference them;
  • add a codex entry;
  • let the journal say "the Adeptus Amasecus establishment inside Martyr's Endurance."

One sentence.

No handholding.

No idiot arrow.

Just consistent nouns.

Every time I had to answer Eugene with "the game calls it X in the quest and Y on the map," a tiny servoskull inside me screamed.
