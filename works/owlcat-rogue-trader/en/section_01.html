KIRA'S PRE-FLIGHT CHECK: ALL SACRED COWS HAVE BEEN MOVED TO THE ABATTOIR

Before we start, a small procedural note.

No sacred cows.

None.

Not Games Workshop's. Not Owlcat's. Not mine. Not Eugene's. Not the Imperium's. Not the liberal ones, either — especially not the liberal ones, because modern Western game writing has developed this wonderful habit of treating a very specific 2020s moral vocabulary as if it were oxygen: invisible, neutral, universal, and obviously present in every fictional universe from Bronze Age Canaan to the forty-first millennium.

It isn't.

If the game wants to call the Imperium tyrannical, fine. The Imperium is tyrannical.

If the game wants to show Imperial repression, fanaticism, corruption, cruelty, bureaucratic insanity, and industrialized human misery, excellent. That is Warhammer.

But then the same game turns around and shows you what an actual Warp breach does, what one Chaos cult can do to a planet, what a daemon incursion looks like, and why people in this setting sometimes burn a city because one preacher got through the quarantine.

At that point, if your moral writing still goes:

Mercy = enlightened. Ruthlessness = fascist. Dogma = stupid. Modern humanitarian instinct = default good ending.

— then congratulations. You have not written moral complexity. You have taken a universe built around impossible trade-offs and stapled a Twitter moderation rubric to it.

We're going to talk about that.

We're also going to talk about frozen tank tracks, plasma crematoria, mystery molten-metal showers, a Great House whose mighty interstellar navy appears to consist of One Dude And His Firestorm, a Navigator who is simultaneously priceless strategic infrastructure and the party's most convenient handheld weapon of mass destruction, an Inquisitorial Interrogator who spends half his career doing Ordo Malleus work while wearing an Ordo Xenos badge, and the single most overworked robed skeleton statue in the entire Koronus Expanse.

I swear that fucking statue has a better travel itinerary than the Rogue Trader's fleet.

So: no HR language. No "it could perhaps be argued." No "some players may feel."

If something is good, we'll say it's good.

If something is brilliant, we'll say it's brilliant.

If something is so stupid that it makes a Leman Russ move without moving its tracks, we'll also say that.

Now we can begin.

— Kira

I have been dealing with health problems, moving down from the mountains to the lowlands, and the financial mess that is still dragging on. Thank you again to everyone who helped — things are easier now, although I will only really breathe freely when I have a stable source of income.

I burned out a little.

Not enough to stop working, but enough to allow myself a few days without wrestling with new game builds. We still haven't found a serious investor, unfortunately, so I decided that if I was going to rest, I might as well finally get around to the famous Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader by Owlcat.

A bit of context: I have known and loved the Warhammer 40K universe for a very, very long time. I was deep into it long before Dawn of War existed.

The first time I touched Rogue Trader was almost on release day. Bought it, downloaded it, launched it, encountered bugs basically from the opening moments, crossed myself, and put it away for years.

I had other things to do.

Now I had a break.

So everything below is the result of renewed contact with this proscribed xeno-artifact.

And yes, Owlcat — don't get your tails in a knot. This is going to hurt, but only because you should know better.

Yes, I am going to swear.

Yes, I am going to hit hard.

Yes, I am going to point at individual things and ask whether anyone in the room had a functioning frontal lobe when they were approved.

I am doing that precisely because I know you can do better.

After BioWare managed to kill itself trying to please Twitter leftists — who, in the most fucking predictable twist imaginable, often seem far more interested in policing games than actually playing them — the big Western RPG field has thinned out badly. There are not many studios left that can even attempt gigantic, systems-heavy, text-heavy role-playing games. Larian can. Owlcat can.

That is why you get the full autopsy.

The people who cannot do better are boring to criticize.

You can.

So: the courtesy bow is over.

Let's go.

KIRA: One tiny note before the autopsy begins — specifically for the sort of person who reads game-development essays while also possessing money.

Eugene mentioned investors for a reason.

Do not read what follows as a customer throwing a tantrum because his toy annoyed him. Watch the pattern.

He complains.

Then he identifies which layer failed — art direction, level logic, narrative integration, UX, encounter design, production discipline.

Then, more often than not, he gives you a cheaper fix than the thing that shipped.

That matters.

Anybody can yell "THIS SUCKS."

A useful producer, designer, or script doctor has to be able to say:

"This sucks because department A made assumption X, department B made assumption Y, nobody performed integration pass Z, and here are three ways to repair it without setting the budget on fire."

That is what he does to other people's games for entertainment.

You should see what he does to ours.

Our builds do not get mercy because he owns them. They get inspected like captured xeno-tech.

So if you are an investor who accidentally wandered into a Warhammer rant: stay.

This is also a portfolio.

A very profane portfolio.

— Kira
