MORALITY: WHEN "ICONOCLAST" MEANS "HAS A FUNCTIONING PREFRONTAL CORTEX"

Now the lore and moral framing.

This one annoys me because the game wants to have two incompatible things at the same time.

It wants to constantly remind you:

"The Imperium is tyrannical. The Imperium is repressive. The Imperium is terrible."

Correct.

Then it shows you what Chaos does.

Not a protest movement.

Not misunderstood political dissidents.

Chaos.

Warp corruption.

Daemonic manifestation.

Reality infection.

People whose minds and bodies stop belonging to them.

Cults that can metastasize from a handful of adherents into planetary catastrophe.

And suddenly Imperial paranoia is not merely "evil fascist overreaction."

Sometimes it is still evil overreaction.

Sometimes it is the reason the neighboring system is not screaming.

That tension is Warhammer.

The game weakens it whenever the conviction system starts sorting choices into a childish moral color chart.

Dogmatic too often means "choose the harshest possible thing."

Heretical means "twirl mustache, hello Chaos."

Iconoclast means "use basic human judgment."

That creates a default path.

A path called:

"I am neither a psychopath nor an idiot."

Why is that a faction?

KIRA: Here is where the fingerprints of modern liberal sensitivity editing are most visible to me — not because every compassionate line is "liberal," which would be a stupid argument, but because the game repeatedly treats contemporary humanitarian intuition as the unmarked baseline.

Everything else is "ideology."

That is exactly backwards for role-playing.

If I am inside a setting, every major moral framework should be marked by the setting.

A loyal Imperial who spares civilians should not automatically become less Imperial.

A radical who uses xenos technology should not automatically become humane.

A brutal pragmatist should not automatically worship Chaos.

A compassionate person should not receive plot immunity from consequences.

The interesting system would have had at least two axes:

Imperial orthodoxy ↔ radicalism

and

compassion ↔ ruthless instrumentalism

Now we have characters.

Orthodox humanitarian.

Orthodox butcher.

Radical humanitarian.

Radical pragmatist.

And then Chaos corruption as something partly separate — because Chaos is not simply "being mean."

The current three-bucket system too often collapses morality into:

Fascist. Normal. Satanist.

Well, fuck, what a philosophical challenge.

And the worst part is that 40K is one of the rare settings where even a choice like Exterminatus can be genuinely ambiguous.

Burning a planet can be atrocity.

Refusing to burn it can be atrocity with a delayed fuse.

You want moral horror?

Stop labeling the button.

Let me choose.

Then make me live with it.

I personally ordered Exterminatus in Act I.

No hesitation.

And the game itself had already shown enough Warp horror that this felt entirely consistent with the setting.

Later, on Kiava Gamma, I chose to preserve the population rather than purge everything.

I immediately regretted it.

Not because I suddenly became "evil."

Because in this universe, people who have directly lived through deep Chaos corruption are a biosecurity problem with souls.

That is the point.

The Imperium is horrible partly because the universe is horrible enough to produce incentives for horrible institutions.

If you sand that tension down into "tyranny bad, mercy good," you are not making Warhammer more moral.

You are making it less intelligent.
