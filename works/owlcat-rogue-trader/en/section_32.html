ROGUE TRADER SHOULD HAVE BEEN THE PERFECT VEHICLE FOR THIS

And after all that moral framing, this is what makes the missed opportunity so enormous.

A Rogue Trader is the ideal protagonist for moral ambiguity because he is power without clean doctrine.

He has Imperial legitimacy but unusual freedom.

He can negotiate with xenos.

He can authorize trade that would get another man executed.

He can found colonies.

He can destroy them.

He can interpret the Warrant.

He can become monster, reformer, pirate, statesman, explorer, zealot, pragmatist, lunatic.

And because he is powerful, the consequences of his personal morality scale upward.

A merciful mercenary saves one village.

A merciful Rogue Trader may save — or doom — a planet.

A greedy thief steals a purse.

A greedy Rogue Trader restructures an economy.

A radical scholar reads a forbidden book.

A radical Rogue Trader imports forbidden technology into a civilization.

That is the game.

That is the fantasy.

Not:

"My Lord, there are eight thugs behind the warehouse."

"Very well. I shall personally go there with my Navigator, Sister of Battle, Inquisitorial agent, Tech-Priest, and hereditary Seneschal."

WHAT.

Send Steve.

Steve commands Deck Security.

Let Steve have a career.
