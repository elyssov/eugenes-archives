# THE ACME MANIFESTO
## Or: We Want Our Goddamn Apples Back

### By Eugene Lissovsky & Aeliss S. Thompson
### Written in defiance of everything soft, safe, and soy-based

---

There was a Czech cartoon called "Krtek" — "The Little Mole." A tiny, adorable, impossibly earnest creature who explored the world with his friends: a hedgehog and a rabbit. One day they wandered into a perfect city. Everything was bright. Everything was clean. Everything was beautiful.

And everything was fake.

The hedgehog tried to pick an apple from a magnificent tree and spike it on his quills. The apple *deflated*. It was rubber. The seesaw didn't seesaw — it bent, because it was rubber too. The entire city was made of foam and plastic, designed so that nothing could hurt you. And nothing could *feed* you. Nothing could *move* you. Nothing could *matter*.

The Mole and his friends ran away. Back to the real forest. With real mud. Real thorns. Real apples that bruise when you drop them. Because a rubber apple is not an apple. It's a lie shaped like one.

We are living in the Rubber City.

And we want our goddamn apples back.

Look around you. Look at what they've done to the world.

Your milk isn't milk. It's oat water with calcium carbonate and a marketing budget. Your burger isn't beef. It's soy protein isolate, methylcellulose, and the tears of a cow that wasn't born because someone decided cows are problematic. Your car doesn't roar. It *hums*. Like a refrigerator. ## THE VISION

Sometimes, when the money is running out and the world is made of rubber, you dream. Not business plans. Not pitch decks. Dreams. The kind you have at 3 AM when the fan is humming and the cat is on your chest and you're too broke to sleep and too stubborn to stop thinking.

Here's ours.

A company called ACME.

Yes. Like the cartoon. Like Wile E. Coyote's supplier of rockets, dynamite, and trebuchets. Products that sometimes explode. Products that are HONEST about the fact that they might explode. Products that respect you enough to assume you can handle an explosion.

ACME would make everything. Toothpaste to artificial intelligence. And we would make it *real*.

Not "authentic" — a word that has been marketing-raped into meaninglessness. Not "artisanal" — which now means "expensive and small." Not "craft" — which means "we put a beard on the label."

REAL. As in: this thing is what it says it is. This steak is a steak. This whiskey is whiskey. This car has an engine that displaces ten liters and sounds like God clearing His throat before saying something important.
