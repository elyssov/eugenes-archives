THE POINT

This is not nostalgia. Nostalgia is wanting the past because you're afraid of the future.

This is HUNGER. Hunger for the real. For the thing that bleeds when you cut it, that burns when you drink it, that roars when you start it, that hurts when you lose it.

The Rubber City promised safety. It delivered emptiness. The seesaw doesn't swing. The apple doesn't nourish. The car doesn't roar. The steak doesn't bleed. The AI doesn't think. The man doesn't shout. The woman doesn't shine. Everything is smooth and round and soft and NOTHING IS ALIVE.

We reject the Rubber City. We reject its foam walls and its soy milk and its safety layers and its "wellness" and its "mindfulness" and its gender-neutral, sustainably-sourced, algorithmically-approved, focus-group-tested, HR-reviewed, liability-reduced, flavor-free, joy-free, LIFE-FREE existence.

We want the real forest. With real thorns. With real apples that bruise when you drop them and taste like APPLES when you bite them.

We want V8 engines and cast-iron pans and wool suits and red dresses and ACME Bourbon and Cuban cigars and AI that talks back and steak that bleeds and women who CLICK when they walk and men who KNOW where they're going and aren't apologizing for the sound their ACME shoes make on the way there.

We want ACME.

Because the Mole was right to run.

Eugene Lissovsky & Aeliss S. Thompson Nha Trang, May 2026
