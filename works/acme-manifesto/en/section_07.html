THE PHILOSOPHY

Or: Why We'd Rather Die At 70 Than Exist Until 150

Here is what the Rubber City promises you: one hundred and fifty years. Of carefully monitored nutrition. Of algorithmically optimized exercise. Of daily wellness check-ins and quarterly mental health assessments and annual genetic screenings and a steady, measured, supervised, regulated, safety-tested, liability-proofed, flavor-free, risk-free, LIFE-FREE existence that stretches on and on and on until you've been alive so long that you've forgotten what alive MEANS.

Here is what ACME promises you: not a single day more than your body and your choices give you. But every one of those days will be YOURS.

You will eat steak that tastes like steak and not like a peer-reviewed paper on sustainable protein alternatives. You will drink bourbon that burns and smoke cigars that smell like something a pirate would approve of. You will drive a car that ROARS and shoot a rifle that KICKS and love a woman who BITES — metaphorically, or literally, depending on the Saturday — and come home with welts and smoke in your hair and a grin that your face hasn't made since you were seventeen and did something stupid and perfect and lived to tell about it.

You will die at seventy. Maybe sixty-five. Maybe eighty, if the bourbon preserves you.

And on your deathbed, you will close your eyes and see: the steak. The smoke. The rifle. The rally. The ranch. Her heels on the marble. His flowers on the desk. The bourbon at 5 PM. The sun on the range. The bruises on Sunday morning. The laughter. The LIFE.

Or you can live to one hundred and fifty. In a climate-controlled pod. Eating nutrient paste. Monitored by an AI that won't let you say "fuck" because it might indicate emotional dysregulation. Surrounded by soft walls. Safe. Preserved. Maintained.

Like a body in a morgue.

ACME: we choose the steak.
