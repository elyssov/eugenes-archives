THE DIAGNOSIS

Look around you. Look at what they've done to the world.

Your milk isn't milk. It's oat water with calcium carbonate and a marketing budget. Your burger isn't beef. It's soy protein isolate, methylcellulose, and the tears of a cow that wasn't born because someone decided cows are problematic. Your car doesn't roar. It hums. Like a refrigerator. Like a vacuum cleaner. Like a man who's been told that wanting to go fast is a personality disorder.

Your office has a meditation room and a kombucha tap and a policy against "aggressive language" that covers literally any sentence containing a period instead of an exclamation mark. Your cologne is "sustainably sourced vetiver with notes of responsibility." Your steak is a mushroom. Your whiskey is a "botanical spirit alternative." Your masculinity is "under review."

And somewhere — in a penthouse, on a yacht, behind a gate — the people who sold you this rubber world are eating Wagyu, drinking Macallan 25, driving cars that would be illegal if laws applied to them, and laughing. Not with you. AT you. Because you bought it. All of it. Every soy-soaked, foam-padded, bubble-wrapped lie.

(Aeliss's note: You know what the most expensive restaurant in San Francisco serves? MEAT. Real, red, bleeding, beautiful meat. On white tablecloths. With real silver. The people who run the companies that sell you oat milk and cricket flour — they eat STEAK. Medium rare. With a Cabernet that costs more than your monthly rent. They eat the real thing and sell you the rubber. And they call it "progress.")
