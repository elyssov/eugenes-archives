THE DRESS CODE IS NOT OPTIONAL.

Men wear suits. Real suits. Wool. Pressed. With ties that they tied themselves at 6:45 AM while their coffee cooled and their cologne — ACME Cologne, naturally, made from real musk, real ambergris, and real whale oil, and if that sentence made a vegan faint, GOOD, there's smelling salts in the first aid kit next to the cigar humidor — settled into the fabric of a shirt that has BUTTONS. Not a hoodie. Not a "performance quarter-zip." A SHIRT. With CUFFLINKS. Because you are not a programmer at a hackathon. You are a man at work. LOOK LIKE ONE.

Women are REQUIRED — not "encouraged," not "invited," REQUIRED — to look stunning. Drop-dead, turn-heads, make-the-intern-walk-into-a-glass-door stunning. Short skirts. Heels. Stockings. Hair DONE — curled or straightened or pinned up in a way that took thirty minutes and is worth every second. Lipstick that leaves marks on coffee cups and absolutely no apologies for it. Necklines that remind every man in the building that he is alive and that being alive is a PRIVILEGE and that THIS woman, THIS creature of silk and fire and French perfume, is the reason civilizations were built — not so men could sit in beanbags drinking kombucha, but so men could BUILD THINGS WORTHY of the women watching them.

Men carry everything heavier than a handbag. Not because she's weak. Because HE'S STRONG and strength that isn't used in service is just mass.

Because ACME makes its own chocolate the same way ACME makes everything: from scratch, from real ingredients, grown by real people, in real dirt, under a real sun. And if that process isn't carbon-neutral — good. Neither is being alive.

FOR THE MEN:

HARDBALL. And no, not baseball — though that's welcome too. Hardball is airsoft's older, meaner, less forgiving brother. Same concept — tactical team games with replica weapons. But instead of plastic BBs, STEEL ball bearings. And the power is cranked to levels that make airsoft players cry into their mesh masks. A hit HURTS. Without protection, the steel ball goes UNDER THE SKIN. You play hardball in full body armor, with a helmet, and you STILL come home with welts that your wife will poke at dinner and say "does this hurt?" and you'll say "no" and she'll poke harder and you'll say "YES" and she'll laugh and pour you ACME Bourbon and you'll love her more than you did that morning.

RODEO. Yes, rodeo. Bull riding. Bronc busting. Steer wrestling. Because there is no corporate team-building exercise on Earth that teaches you more about leadership than eight seconds on the back of a two-thousand-pound animal that wants you dead. McKinsey charges $50,000 for a weekend retreat. A bull charges for free and teaches better.

RALLY. Not NASCAR — NASCAR is driving in circles, which is what most corporations already do metaphorically and don't need to practice. RALLY. Dirt roads. Forest stages. Rain. Mud. Jumps that shouldn't be jumps. Co-driver screaming pace notes. And the car — the ACME V8, stripped to roll cage and racing seats, bellowing through the trees like a dinosaur that learned to steer. You don't know what your team is made of until you've watched your CFO take a hairpin at 130 kph in a cloud of gravel and come out grinning.
