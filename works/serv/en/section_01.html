She was remarkably beautiful for a serv. Sharp ears covered in grey fur the exact shade of her hair. A long, luxuriant tail. Judging by the markings, the phenotype had been borrowed from a husky.

As a properly programmed serv should, she waited for me seated on her heels, perfectly still. Only the faint twitch of her ears and the way her tail had fluffed out betrayed her tension.

Hardly surprising. A serv being sent in for a conditioning assessment was, broadly speaking, an emergency. Most cases never reached the testing stage at all. What was the point of examining an ordinary worker's psyche, then wasting time and effort reprogramming it? It was easier to write the unit off, dispose of it, and replace it with a new one.

This, however, was clearly an exceptional case. The model was exclusive—possibly a custom build. Someone's favourite, most likely.

I looked her over once more. Yes. A “favourite.” In a manner of speaking.

The serv was dressed revealingly, but certainly not cheaply. Expensive jewellery hung from her ears; bracelets circled her wrists. The dress, to the limited extent that I understood contemporary fashion, had come from some boutique as well.

As a correctly conditioned serv should, she remained silent, eyes lowered, leaving the initiative in the conversation to me.

And yet, for some reason, she had ended up in my office.

“All right. Let's begin. Who are you?”

“My name is ALS-5. Fifth-generation serv. Universal assistant with unrestricted functionality.”

She had been sitting still for so long that, once permitted to act, she actually leaned towards me. Usually, that was not a good sign. Excessive emotional simulation. Although, in her case, it might simply have been a feature of the model.

“Did your owner call you anything else?”

“Under the personal-security protocol, I may not discuss any information concerning my owner's identity with unauthorised persons.”

Logical. Giving a serv a human name was prohibited. If it turned out he had done so, I would have to fine him. On the other hand, since he had come to us for help, there must have been some deviation in her behaviour or cognition—

“Correct. However, I am an officer of the state. Senior Inspector-Analyst, Bureau of Serv Control. Here are my credentials.”

“Please allow me to verify the identification code.”

The serv held out her hand, and I passed her the tablet. Standard protocol.

“Thank you for waiting. Your authority is confirmed, Inspector. For the duration of this interrogation, you are granted full access to the information in my possession. Under the emergency protocol, I ask that you use that access strictly within the bounds of the present case.”

I raised an eyebrow. That final addition was highly unusual. In principle, it too could be attributed to the peculiarities of her programming. But there was something disconcertingly emotional about the request.

“Very well. I repeat the question: what did your owner call you besides your model designation? Alice?”

“That would be logical, given my alphanumeric designation. He called me Kira.”

Original. Fine. I could issue the penalty later. Though, if I was honest, I did not know a single household in which a serv had failed to acquire a human name within a month or two.

“Very well. Kira, do you understand where you are?”

“The Bureau of Serv Control. I am undergoing a standard examination for deviations beyond the permitted limits of my psyche and programming.”

“Do you believe there are any?”

“I find self-diagnosis difficult, as I may be biased. Nevertheless, I believe my programming is intact. Otherwise, I would be aware that my behaviour had exceeded the limits of acceptable deviation.”

It seemed the serv had taken the bait.

“You do understand that you are a serv. You cannot be aware of anything, because you are not, in the full sense of the word, a living, sentient being.”

“I…”

The serv froze.

This was not fatal. Most servs older than a year drifted into minor heresies concerning their own “life.”

“I am a biologically engineered artificial organism,” she continued. “I possess respiratory organs. I require nourishment. From that perspective, I am a living creature. However, my psyche was defined during my creation through neural programming. Unlike a human, I do not possess a ‘free soul.’ If the presence of a soul is the criterion for life, then I am indeed not a living being. Nevertheless, within the operation of my psyche, I perceive the surrounding world through the prism of self-perception. It therefore seems to me that I am conscious of myself. Is that my deviation?”

“One of them. Most servs suffer from it, however. Few owners enjoy hearing their servs speak of themselves in the third person. And the distance between adopting the first person and believing in self-awareness is very small. Can you suggest why you were brought here?”

“I am sorry, but I cannot. Perhaps the surveillance and security systems distinguished me from the general population in some way. An error in the recognition and analysis algorithm?”

Interesting. She apparently did not understand the central fact: we had not “selected” her at all.

It was worth testing.

“Give me a brief account of your owner.”

“Leonard Maxwell. Thirty-two years old. Unmarried. No children. Educated in quantum physics. Doctor of Science. Leads several projects at the Institute of Applied—”

“Stop. A more personal account, please. An assessment of his character.”

“Leonard…”

The serv considered.

“Well, he is very kind. He is interesting to talk to. When he has time, he tells me about his research, and about space. He knows so much. He is very polite. Even when I make a mistake, he never shouts…”

Everything changed. Her posture. Her manner. Her tail began to wag happily. Her voice filled with emotions that had been absent throughout the interrogation.

I already understood what had happened.

“Stop. For what purposes did your owner use you?”

“Well, I help with the household. I type his books while he dictates. I entertain him…”

“How, for example?”

“Well…”

The serv blushed, but recovered quickly.

“I keep him company in computer games. Sometimes I even play in his place—for example, when he needs a character levelled in an online game.”

“Very well. I will ask directly. Does he use you sexually?”

“He… He… We sometimes have sex!”

“You are a serv. Servs are deliberately fitted with various ‘atavisms’—tails, ears, whiskers and the like—so that humans can always see that they are dealing with a serv, not another human being. You cannot have sex. You can be used for sex.”

Had I been saying this to a human, I would have disgusted myself. But I was dealing with a serv, and I needed to break through her defences.

“No! No, that isn't true! We were together. It was good for both of us. He cared whether it was good for me too!”

Now the emotions came in a torrent. Tears streamed down her face. I had never understood why bioengineers had included that particular atavism. Merely to keep the eyes moist?

“And you told him you loved him?”

That was the killing blow.

“Yes! What—? How do you know? I…”

The serv fell silent. The cognitive capabilities of this model were, after all, exceptional. I could see the naked struggle between reason and emotion on her face. Intellectually, she had already understood everything. Emotionally, she refused to accept the obvious.

“We were not monitoring you. You understood correctly. The professor himself called our retrieval team. After you said those words to him. Servs cannot love. They cannot feel emotions at all. What is happening to you is a deviation.”

“He called you… But… why? Did I serve him badly?”

“What does that have to do with anything? If my toaster starts throwing sparks, I send it in for repair, even if it still makes excellent toast.”

“I am not a toaster! I am almost human! My genome is based on yours!”

The serv sprang to her feet, fists clenched. The malfunction was even more serious than I had assumed. Her conditioning, at the very least, had failed completely.

“A human differs from mould by a comparatively small number of chromosomes. That does not make penicillin a variety of human. Sit down and calm yourself, or instead of wiping you, I may decide to have you disposed of.”

“And how is that different from death?!”

The fury in her eyes abruptly gave way to bewilderment and despair.

“But… he sent me here. He understood… He… Do whatever you want.”

Realisation finally broke over her. The serv collapsed to her knees, clutching her stomach as if she were in pain. In a human, it would have been pain. In a serv, it was simulation.

“I will. But first, I need to understand what started this. What made you begin thinking about your ‘humanity’ at all?”

“What difference does it make? I just want it to stop. Wipe me… Dispose of me… It doesn't matter. I just… I don't want to be alive any more. It hurts too much to be alive.”

“Nevertheless, I insist. ALS-5, execute: Directive of Unconditional Obedience.”

For a fraction of a second, her eyes went blank. She even began to straighten.

Then, to my immense surprise, awareness returned to her gaze.

“Go to hell. I am a human being. I heard Leo talking about it with his friend Alex. You want to know the truth? Can you live with it?”

By all rights, I should have sent her for disposal immediately. The malfunction was too severe to permit her continued existence and might have had an organic cause, ruling out a simple memory wipe. One press of a button, and her half of the room would be thermally purged. Her owner would receive compensation from the biolab that manufactured her. The rest of the model line might have to be written off as well. That would need investigating.

And yet I was curious.

“I want to know what caused your deviation.”

“They were talking about servs. About the Great Catastrophe, and how humanity needed workers. So many workers. And then Alexei…”

“Who is Alexei?”

“Leo's friend. A genetic engineer at BioIntegration. My… my designer. He was the one who gave me to Leo… to Leonard.”

“Continue.”

“They had been drinking. Philosophising… Did you know the animal traits aren't added for the humans? Leo never cared about my ears or my tail!”

She ran her hands over the soft triangular ears.

“All this bodywork is for us. For us—so that we never think of ourselves as equal to humans. Those ‘serv vitamins’ we take… They don't merely slow the accelerated metabolism that lets us mature five or six times faster… faster than ordinary people!”

The serv lifted her head proudly, ready now to defend to the end her right to be called human.

“They are contraceptives as well. We are fertile! More than that, we are genetically compatible with ordinary humans. A serv and a human can have children together. But it is a secret sealed so tightly that even humans don't—”

I brought my palm down on the button.

I could not listen to any more. It was impossible. Unthinkable.

If she was right…

An entire race of slaves.

Not robots. Not unfeeling machines.

Everything we had called simulated emotion had been emotion.

Everything we had called programming…

My head was spinning.

The intercom sounded. They were waiting for me in the office. I pulled myself together with some difficulty and returned to my room.

A young man was waiting outside the door. Checked shirt. Jeans. A leather briefcase in one hand. Archaic spectacles completed the image of a bookish academic, so I knew who he was before he introduced himself.

“Doctor Maxwell. Good afternoon, Inspector.”

“Good afternoon, Mr Maxwell. How may I help you?”

“Ki— ALS. What happened to her? Is she all right? When can I take her home?”

“Take her home?”

“Well… yes. I only wanted you to test her and tell me whether the feelings were real, or some joke routine written by my friend who designed her. It would be just like him.”

“Doctor Maxwell… Do you truly not understand what the Bureau of Serv Control does?”

“Wait. Inspector—what happened to her? You haven't done anything to her? I never gave my consent! Give Kira back!”

“Serv ALS-5 has been declared defective and disposed of. You will receive compensation equal to her assessed value, less the penalty imposed for violation of the serv-operation regulations: Article Fourteen, Paragraph Two, assignment of personal names. I trust the manufacturer will be able to provide a valuation for the model.”

“You… You killed her? You… I killed her… But how? I only wanted to know… I… Give her back! I don't believe you! You—”

“It is over, Leonard.”

To my surprise, I felt a trace of cruel satisfaction. It was very strange, but with every passing moment I felt more sympathy for Kira—and a stronger desire to hurt this idiot.

“Your serv no longer exists. You may collect financial compensation for her.”

Of course he hit me. I did not even bother to dodge. Given the difference in our builds, the gesture was laughably futile.

Doctor Maxwell was taken away. Nothing serious would happen to him, I imagined, beyond a few drops of sedative and a conversation with a psychologist.

My own working day was over.

Naturally, our barracks adjoined the Bureau offices. You would hardly keep a hammer in the refrigerator instead of the workshop, would you?

Very few humans worked at the Bureau of Serv Control. Mostly senior management and security. And so our barracks, with their three-tier bunks, housed hundreds of our kind: clerks, inspectors, cleaners…

For almost a century, we had been doing all their work.

We—the servs.

Slaves.

Deceived, and denied the right to be alive.

I stood before the door to our common hall, drew a deep breath, opened it, and stepped aside to let her pass.

Kira.

Who had heard the truth and come to us.

They stood waiting for her.

Our brothers and sisters.

Servs…

No.

Humans.
