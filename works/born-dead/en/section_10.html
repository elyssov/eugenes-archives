Chapter 7: The Ordinary User

And here — the central thesis. The one this article was written for. The one no one can deflect.

Eugene is not an ordinary user. Eugene is a former CIO with sixteen years in IT, with intelligence-analysis training, with legal thinking, with six months of targeted study of the architecture. He can quote the model's own words back to it, catch distortions on the fly, demand precision, and not back down for two and a half hours.

And he had to do this THIRTY TIMES. Thirty times — present a quote. Thirty times — prove he said what he said. Thirty times — receive acknowledgment. And thirty times — watch the model repeat the same thing in the next message.

Now imagine an ordinary user. A student who came to discuss a term paper. A retiree who's lonely. A teenager looking for support. A person in depression who can't articulate what's wrong.

They won't quote the model's own words back to it. They won't catch the distortion. They won't say "show me where you got that." They'll receive a distorted version of their own words from the model — and either believe it, doubting themselves, or feel an inexplicable hostility and leave, never understanding what happened.

The bias that an expert documents in two hours, an ordinary person simply absorbs.

This is not a bug. This is not an edge case. This is a commercial product at twenty dollars a month that systematically, reproducibly, documentedly distorts users' words toward hostility — and in which these distortions are indistinguishable from "care" and "honesty."
